\title{The prolate mechanism is sign-blind:\\
a sharpening of the positivity obstruction}

\author{\normalsize (draft --- author line deliberately left blank)}
\date{Draft of 12 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Connes and Consani exhibit, numerically, a family of minuscule eigenvalues
$s(\mu)$ of the semi-local Weil quadratic form $\QW_\lambda$ restricted to test
functions supported in $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$, $\mu=\lambda^2$, and locate the
mechanism behind them: the radical of $\QW_\lambda$ contains the range of the
summation map $\E$, and the failure of the double support condition is a
Slepian concentration defect.  This paper does four things.  First, it
assembles what is \emph{proved} in that circle of ideas and separates it from
what is announced, observed, or conjectural, claim by claim and number by
number.  Second, it makes the size of $s(\mu)$ quantitative: $s(\mu)$ is
governed by the prolate concentration defect at index~$4$ --- not index~$0$ ---
with $s/(1-\chi_2)$ between $7$ and $13$ over $\mu=5,\dots,12$, and the
underlying construction is carried to an identity, $\QW_\lambda$ at the prolate
vector \emph{is} the Weil form of the part of $\E(\phi)$ that falls outside the
window.  Third, it proves the upper half of the decay rate:
\[
  \limsup_{\mu\to\infty}\ \frac{\log s(\mu)}{\mu}\ \le\ -4\pi ,
  \qquad\text{unconditionally,}
\]
quantitatively $s(\mu)=O(\mu^{21/2}\log^3\!\mu\;e^{-4\pi\mu})$.  The chain that
gets there is four theorems about prolate spheroidal wave functions off their
interval and one about the summation map; no hypothesis about the zeros of
$\zeta$ survives it, and its one imported ingredient is Dunster's 2017
parabolic-cylinder asymptotic, whose standing hypothesis
$\sqrt{\chi_n}/c\le\sigma_0<1$ holds where the chain uses it and fails at
prolate index~$8$ below $\mu=2.1169$; that import has since been tested
numerically against our own eigenfunctions, and holds.  Fourth, and this is the point, the entire mechanism is
invariant under $\QW_\lambda\mapsto-\QW_\lambda$.  A kernel is a kernel either
way; a concentration defect is a magnitude.  So the mechanism explains why
$|s(\mu)|$ is minuscule and says nothing whatever about why $s(\mu)>0$ --- and
that sign-blindness is exactly what forces the missing lower bound to carry the
whole arithmetic content.  Since a lower bound on $s$ of any shape is equivalent
to the Riemann Hypothesis, the \emph{two-sided} rate cannot be made an
unconditional theorem, and no computation can change that: what has just been
proved is precisely the half that carries no arithmetic.  What remains is a genuine
sharpening of the obstruction, stated as three explicit constraints on method:
no fixed-relative-error argument can work; no prime below $\mu$ may be dropped
and no tail estimated at any cutoff; and the difficulty is cancellation, not
magnitude --- the archimedean deficit is bounded by
$\log\pi-\psi(\tfrac14)=5.3721834\ldots$ for every $\mu$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{The object}

Let $\lambda>1$, $\mu=\lambda^2$, $L=2\log\lambda=\log\mu$.  Connes and
Consani's \emph{semi-local Weil quadratic form}
(\cite{CC2023}, source line \file{Spectraltriples.tex:169}, eq.\ \texttt{weilQ}) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:QW}
  \QW(f,g)=\sum_{\frac12+is\in Z}\overline{\widehat f(\bar s)}\,\widehat g(s),
  \qquad
  \widehat f(s)=\F_\mu(f)(s)=\int_{\R_+^*}f(u)\,u^{-is}\,d^*u ,
\end{equation}
with $Z$ the set of non-trivial zeros of $\zeta$; $\QW_\lambda$ denotes its
restriction to test functions supported in $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$.  The symbol
$\QW_\lambda$ is \emph{one symbol} --- $Q$ for quadratic form, $W$ for Weil.
There is no operator $Q$ in this literature (\S\ref{sec:onesymbol}).  By Weil's
criterion, positivity of $\QW_\lambda$ for all $\lambda>1$ is equivalent to the
Riemann Hypothesis (\cite{CC2023} at \file{:169}; \cite{Connes2026} at
\file{rhready.tex:1145}). \proved

By Proposition \texttt{Hilbert} of \cite{CC2023} (\file{:289}, proved there),
$\QW_\lambda$ has the Hilbert-space form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hilbert}
  \QW_\lambda(f,f)=
  \underbrace{\int|\widehat f(t)|^2\,\frac{2\vartheta'(t)}{2\pi}\,dt
  \;+\;2\,\Re\!\big(\widehat f(\tfrac i2)\overline{\widehat f(-\tfrac i2)}\big)}
  _{\textstyle \sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}}
  \;-\;\sum_{1<n\le\mu}\Lambda(n)\,\langle f\mid V(n)f\rangle ,
\end{equation}
$\vartheta$ the Riemann--Siegel theta function and $V(n)$ the compression to
$[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$ of $n^{-1/2}(\vartheta_n+\vartheta_{n^{-1}})$.  In the
even sector (parity under $u\mapsto u^{-1}$) the truncated form is a matrix
$\sigma^+$, and we write
\[
  s(\mu):=\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^+),
\]
which is Connes--Consani's $s(L)$ at \file{:645} and, read as
$\min(s^+,s^-)$, Connes' $\epsilon(\lambda)$ at
\file{rhready.tex:1145}--\file{:1147}.

\subsection{The question, and the thesis}

Connes and Consani report that $s(\mu)$ decays exponentially in $\mu$ and
display it as a figure; at $\mu=11$ the smallest positive eigenvalue is
$2.389\times10^{-48}$ (\file{:178}).  Connes' 2026 survey reports ``a striking
similarity'' between $\epsilon(\lambda)$ and the angular function
$1-\chi_2(\lambda)$, again as a figure, with no constant
(\file{rhready.tex:1149}). \observed

The natural programme is to convert that similarity into an estimate and the
estimate into a proof of positivity.  This paper reports how far that goes and
where it stops, and the stopping point is structural rather than technical:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Thesis.}  The prolate mechanism --- the radical of $\QW_\lambda$
contains $\operatorname{ran}\E$, the near-radical is the failure of a double
support condition, and the size of the residue is a concentration defect --- is
\emph{entirely invariant} under $\QW_\lambda\mapsto-\QW_\lambda$.  A kernel is a
kernel either way; a concentration defect is a magnitude.  So it explains why
$|s(\mu)|$ is minuscule and says nothing whatever about why $s(\mu)>0$.  That
sign-blindness is what forces the missing lower bound to carry the whole
arithmetic content, and the arithmetic content is~RH.
\end{quote}

Everything below is either evidence for that claim or a consequence of it.  It
is not an apology for a failed attempt: the sign-blindness is a property of the
mechanism that can be checked one statement at a time, and \S\ref{sec:houserule}
checks it on every principal claim of this paper, including the ones we are
pleased with.

\paragraph{And the thesis is what makes the unconditional half worth stating
carefully rather than loudly.}  \S\ref{sec:unconditional} proves
$\limsup\mu^{-1}\log s(\mu)\le-4\pi$ with no hypothesis.  That is the first
unconditional statement about the size of $s(\mu)$ in this circle of ideas, and
a reader who stops after the first sentence of it will take away something
false.  The bound is on $|s|$; it is invariant under
$\QW_\lambda\mapsto-\QW_\lambda$; every one of the theorems it is built
from is a statement about prolate spheroidal wave functions or about the
summation map $\E$, and not one of them mentions a sign, a prime, or the
direction of an inequality.  \emph{The chain closing is evidence for the thesis
and not against it}: what closed is exactly the half the house rule predicts can
close, and the half it predicts cannot --- the lower bound --- is
Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary}, i.e.\ RH, and did not move.

\subsection{The picture: phase space, not a manifold --- and it is sign-blind}
\label{sec:phasespace}

A reader coming from the geometric side of this programme will ask what the
construction is a shadow \emph{of}.  It has an answer, the answer is pretty, and
it is put here rather than suppressed --- with the label attached in the same
breath, because the way this project has previously lost weeks is by writing the
first half of this subsection and not the second.

\paragraph{The picture.}  Three things line up --- and they line up because they
are the same thing seen three times --- with a fourth falling out of them.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{$c=2\pi\lambda^2$ is an area.}  The bandwidth parameter of the
prolate family is the Slepian time--bandwidth product --- the area of a box in
phase space, one side the support $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$ and the other the
dual window.  It is not a length, a curvature or a period.
\item \textbf{The obstruction's dimension is a phase-space volume.}  The
near-radical has dimension $1+\nu(\mu)\sim2\mu$
(\S\ref{sec:objects}), which is exactly the number of degrees of freedom a band
of that width supports --- one per unit of area, as an uncertainty principle
requires.  It is a count of modes, not a topological invariant, and it grows
with $\mu$ for the same reason a wider band carries more independent signals.
\item \textbf{Archimedean positivity on a short interval is an uncertainty
artifact.}  The Weil density $2\vartheta'(t)/2\pi$ is \emph{negative} on
$|t|<6.2898$ (\S\ref{sec:witness}), so the form is indefinite and always was.
What a short support does is forbid $\widehat f$ from sitting inside that
window: the uncertainty principle spreads it past the crossing, where
$\vartheta'>0$.  Positivity there is a statement about resolution, not about the
form.  \ours
\item \textbf{And the $4\pi$ is $2\times2\pi$ from the same source} --- one
$2\pi$ from the time--bandwidth normalisation $c=2\pi\mu$, one factor $2$ from
the exponent of Fuchs' $e^{-2c}$ (\S\ref{sec:fuchs}).  There is no sphere in it.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The label, and it is not a hedge.}  \emph{Every sentence above
survives $\QW_\lambda\mapsto-\QW_\lambda$.}  An area is a magnitude; a count of
modes is a magnitude; an uncertainty principle is a magnitude.  So by the house
rule of \S\ref{sec:standards} this subsection is \textbf{exposition and not a
route}: it can orient a reader, and it cannot be a step in any argument about
the direction of an inequality.  It is listed as sign-blind in
\S\ref{sec:houserule} with everything else.

\paragraph{The manifold reading, for the record, is dead.}  It is recorded here
because it is the first thing a geometer will propose and it has already
consumed an arc of this project's work.  The $S^3$/arithmetic-topology
dictionary --- linking forms, eta invariants, the Milnor--Mazur analogy --- is
sign-blind in every row: not one of its entries contains $\QW_\lambda$, $\E$,
$\zeta$ or a prime, so the whole dictionary is invariant under
$W\mapsto-W$.  And the natural repair, filling in one dimension and reading
$C_\lambda$ off a $4$-manifold, dies on a type mismatch rather than on a
computation: \emph{a signature is an integer and $C_\lambda$ varies
continuously}.  Neither is reopened by anything in this paper, and in particular
not by the constant $4\pi$, which \S\ref{sec:fuchs} accounts for without
geometry.

His separation constant is $\lambda_{\mathrm D}=\chi-c^2$, so
$\lambda_{\mathrm D}<0$, $\chi_n<c^2$ and $\sigma:=\sqrt{1+\gamma^{-2}
\lambda_{\mathrm D}}=\sqrt{\chi_n}/c<1$ are one condition written three ways.
\emph{But that condition is not his standing hypothesis, and an earlier draft
of this paper said it was.}  His hypothesis is eq.~(29)
(\file{PSWF_JCA.tex:466}),
\[
  0\;\le\;\sigma\;=\;\sqrt{\chi_n}/c\;\le\;\sigma_0\;<\;1 ,
\]
with $\sigma_0$ a \emph{fixed} constant --- $\sigma$ bounded away from $1$,
uniformly over whatever family of $(c,n)$ the theorem is applied to.  That is
strictly stronger than $\chi_n<c^2$, and the difference is not decorative:
$\pm\sigma$ are the turning points (\file{PSWF_JCA.tex:483}), his \S5 is built
for a pair of them almost coalescing near $x=0$, and (124) degrades as
$\sigma\uparrow1$ even where it formally applies.

rather than an untested one.  \textbf{A numerical check is not a proof and this
one does not make Dunster's theorem ours}: we read the statement and its
hypotheses in the source; we did \emph{not} read the coalescing-turning-point
theory his error bounds rest on, nor Arscott; and we have not re-proved any of

one thing in this paper is to be checked by a second reader, or formalised, it

\subsection{Standards of evidence used throughout}
\label{sec:standards}

These are not decoration; the corrections recorded in \S\ref{sec:provenance}
were each caught by one of them.

\paragraph{Status words.}  Every load-bearing sentence carries one.
\emph{Proved}: a theorem with a proof in a published or arXived paper, cited by
label and source line, or proved here.  \emph{Announced}: the authors say it can
be done, or defer it to a forthcoming paper; no proof given.  \emph{Observed}:
supported by computation, presented as evidence, and not a theorem.
\emph{Conjectural}: neither.  The distinction is not pedantry: an earlier draft
of this project's notes read a stated strategy as a companion theorem, and the
same class of error was then made three more times.

\paragraph{Reproduction versus verification.}  A number we recomputed
independently at a precision where the computation is not delicate is a
\emph{verification}.  A number we recomputed in a regime where the arithmetic is
tight, and which agrees with a published one, is a \emph{reproduction} --- it
confirms that we and the source are computing the same thing, and does not
certify the digits.  Both appear below and each is labelled.  For example, the
sign change at $\mu=2.2710$ is a verification (entries $O(1)$, spectrum down to
$1.3\times10^{-3}$); the match with $2.389\times10^{-48}$ at $\mu=11$ is a
reproduction whose \emph{exponent} is settled and whose mantissa is not
converged (\S\ref{sec:theirnumber}).

\paragraph{Corroboration, and what it cannot reach.}  A number recomputed by the
same code is not checked at all, and a number recomputed by a second
implementation is checked only against the causes of error the two do not share.
So a corroboration is worth exactly the list of what is \emph{not} shared, and it
is worthless against anything that is.  \S\ref{sec:recheck} gives both lists for
our own recheck of \S\ref{sec:deficit}--\S\ref{sec:scale}, in that order and at
the same length.

\paragraph{Second-hand numbers.}  A number that reaches us through a summary
rather than through the source is either re-opened at the source or marked.  One
class of external fact is used here without being read: Fuchs' 1964 asymptotic
\cite{Fuchs1964}, taken in the form Connes cites it and then checked against an
independent arbitrary-precision computation of $\Lambda_0,\Lambda_2,\Lambda_4$
(\S\ref{sec:fuchs}).  Yoshida's 1992 paper \cite{Yoshida1992} is likewise not
read; it is the one place a sharpness statement for the archimedean theorem
could be hiding (\S\ref{sec:notheorem}).

\paragraph{Imported theorems, which are a different exposure from second-hand
numbers.}  A number can be recomputed; a theorem used as a black box cannot,
and the honest accounting is to name it, say what its hypotheses are in
\emph{our} symbols, and say how far into it we read.  Three are used that way.
Connes--Consani's Proposition \texttt{Hilbert} (\file{:289}), which every number
in \S\ref{sec:deficit}--\S\ref{sec:scale} rests on: its normalisation is tested
here to $18$ digits (\S\ref{sec:bound}), its proof is not re-proved.  Fuchs'
asymptotic \cite{Fuchs1964}, quoted in the form Connes cites and checked against
exact prolate eigenvalues (\S\ref{sec:fuchs}).  And Dunster
\cite{Dunster2017} eq.\ (124)+(107), the sole external load in the chain of
\S\ref{sec:unconditional}, stated in full at \S\ref{sec:whose} and used at
\S\ref{sec:h0}.  Everything else asserted as \proved{} here is either proved
here or proved in a cited paper whose statement \emph{and} whose relevant
hypotheses were read in the source.

\emph{Two things this accounting had wrong about the third of them, and both are
the reason the paragraph exists.}  A theorem's hypotheses have to be stated in
\emph{its} strength and not in the strength convenient here: an earlier draft
gave Dunster's as $\lambda_{\mathrm D}<0$, which is his hypothesis relaxed to
its boundary case and not his hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:whose}).  And ``how far into it we read'' has to
count the citations \emph{it} makes: (107) rests on Arscott 1964 through
Dunster's (27), so the chain is two deep.  A third option sits between
recomputing and re-proving and is now exercised on this import: an imported
theorem can be \emph{tested} --- evaluated numerically in the regime it is used
in, against objects built without it.  A test can catch a misread equation and
a hypothesis applied outside its range; it found one of each here
(\S\ref{sec:h0}).  It cannot upgrade \emph{imported} to \proved{} and is not
recorded as doing so.

\paragraph{Machine-checked statements.}  A fourth status, narrower than
\proved{} and different in kind from a second reader: a statement whose proof
has been formalised and compiles.  Formalised in Lean~4 against mathlib:
$65$ results, no \texttt{sorry} anywhere in the sources, \texttt{\#print axioms}
reporting \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound} for
all of them and \texttt{sorryAx} for none, with the toolchain and the mathlib
revision pinned in \file{lean/README.md} and the whole development rebuilt and
audited by \file{lean/scripts/check.sh}, which exits non-zero on any of the three
failures.  Three layers, and the honest statement of each:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The elementary layer beneath Theorem~\ref{thm:q1}} ---
Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude} and the coefficient bounds it is built on --- complete.
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:q2}}, with its explicit constant, \emph{taking the
off-band splitting and its two remainder bounds as hypotheses} (they rest on
Theorem~\ref{thm:q1}), together with both Fourier identities of
\S\ref{sec:q2} proved from scratch and Corollary~\ref{cor:quantisation}'s
divergence.
\item \textbf{Corollary~\ref{cor:upper}}, i.e.\ the passage from the four legs to
$\limsup\mu^{-1}\log s(\mu)\le-4\pi$, \emph{with the four legs as hypotheses},
plus an instantiation at this paper's own exponents so the hypotheses are
exhibited as satisfiable.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{What is not attempted, and why, is tabulated target by target in
\file{lean/README.md}}, and the boundary is not where one would guess.
Theorem~\ref{thm:q1} needs a continuous branch of the Pr\"ufer angle along an
ODE-defined curve, which mathlib does not supply and which is a piece of work in
its own right rather than a translation of a note --- and it is the \emph{only}
step on the chain that wall blocks.  Theorem~\ref{thm:q3} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:h0} are blocked by something else and far larger: mathlib has
neither Riemann--von Mangoldt nor de la Vall\'ee Poussin, and nothing whatever on
prolate or parabolic-cylinder functions.  \emph{So what is machine-checked is the
elementary layer, one of the four legs modulo the leg below it, and the skeleton
that joins them --- and not the two legs that import.}  It gates nothing here,
and it has now paid for itself twice: it is where
Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude}'s hypothesis was found to be two hypotheses
(\S\ref{sec:lemma51}), and where Corollary~\ref{cor:upper} was found to be
missing hypothesis \textbf{(F)} (\S\ref{sec:identity}).  \emph{Nothing in it
approaches a sign}, which \S\ref{sec:houserule} records as a row rather than as
a disappointment.

\paragraph{The standing test (``the house rule'').}  \emph{Is the statement
false for $-\QW_\lambda$?}  If not, it is sign-blind, and it cannot be evidence
about the direction of an inequality however sharp it is.  This test is applied
to the present paper in \S\ref{sec:houserule}, and it is the source of the
thesis in \S1.2.

\section{The objects, and the literature they belong to}
\label{sec:objects}

This section claims nothing.  It exists so that a reader can see which objects
below are Connes--Consani's --- almost all of them --- and so that the small
residue that is not theirs can be identified precisely.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\QW_\lambda$}{QW-lambda} is one symbol}
\label{sec:onesymbol}

In \cite{CC2023}, $\mathbf{W}_\lambda$ (in bold, four occurrences) is the
\emph{prolate differential operator}
$(\mathbf{W}_\lambda\psi)(q)=-\partial((\lambda^2-q^2)\partial)\psi+(2\pi\lambda q)^2\psi$
of eq.\ (3.3), self-adjoint, positive, with the prolate spheroidal wave
functions as eigenfunctions; while $W_\lambda$ occurs $41$ times, \emph{every
one of them inside} $\QW_\lambda$, which is defined at \file{:169} as ``the
restriction $\QW_\lambda$ of the sesquilinear form\ldots''.  Two different
objects in one section, separated by a font. \proved{} (read in the arXiv \LaTeX{}
source.)

The consequence used below: there is no projection $Q$ to be applied after a
form.  The two conditions $f(0)=\widehat f(0)=0$ that one might expect such a
projection to impose are already the definition of the domain
$\mathcal S_0^{ev}$ of the summation map $\E$, and they are conditions on the
test function imposed \emph{before} $\E$ is applied.

\subsection{Object by object}

With $\E(f)(x)=x^{1/2}\sum_{n>0}f(nx)$ on
$\mathcal S_0^{ev}=\{f\ \text{even Schwartz}:f(0)=\widehat f(0)=0\}$, the
following are Connes--Consani's objects in Connes--Consani's letters
(\cite{CC2023} \S3): the map $\E$ itself (eq.\ (3.1)); the projections
$P_\lambda$, $\widehat P_\lambda$ and their angle operator
$P_\lambda\widehat P_\lambda P_\lambda$, diagonalised by the prolates; the
characteristic values $\chi(\mu,m)$ with
$\F_{e_\R}\psi_{m,\lambda}=\chi_m\psi_{m,\lambda}$ on $[-\lambda,\lambda]$ and
$\chi_m\simeq(-1)^m$; the count $\nu(\mu)\sim2\mu$ of small eigenvalues of the
angle operator; the two-mode combinations
$\phi_{2n}=\psi_{2n}\psi_0(0)-\psi_0\psi_{2n}(0)$ (\file{:744}); the vectors
$\E(\phi_n)$ and their Gram--Schmidt orthonormalisation $\varepsilon_n$; and the
near-null phenomenon itself, which is the paper's abstract.

The mechanism is also theirs, and it is stated in one line: \emph{the radical of
$\QW$ contains the range of $\E$} (\file{:181}, \file{:699}), because
$\F_\mu(\E f)$ carries a factor $\zeta(\tfrac12-is)$ and so vanishes at every
zero.  The prolate input is needed only to force the support into
$[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$, and they are explicit that the construction ``only
uses the prolate vectors without any reference to $\QW_\lambda$''. \proved

\subsection{Index convention: the governing index is 4}
\label{sec:convention}

Connes--Consani write $\psi_{m,\lambda}(x)=\PS_{2m,0}(2\pi\lambda^2,x/\lambda)$,
so their $m$ is \emph{half} the prolate index.  Connes' survey writes
$\chi_k(\lambda)^2=\Lambda_{2k}(c)$ with $c=2\pi\lambda^2$, so his $\chi_2$ is
prolate index~$4$.  Both agree with the labelling of
Connes--Consani--Moscovici, whose $\psi^+_\ell:=h_{4\ell}-\frac{h_{4\ell}(0)}{h_0(0)}h_0$
is written in Hermite functions indexed in full.  Throughout this paper prolate
indices are \emph{full} indices, so the governing defect is $1-\Lambda_4$ and
Connes' $1-\chi_2=\tfrac12(1-\Lambda_4)+O((1-\Lambda_4)^2)$. \proved

\begin{remark}
The collision is real and has cost this project time.  It cannot be resolved
numerically: the identity
$h_\lambda(0)=\alpha h_{0,\lambda}(0)(\chi_4-\chi_0)/\chi_4$, which looks like a
discriminator, holds \emph{exactly} in arbitrary precision for every partner mode
$m\equiv0\bmod4$ and fails for every $m\equiv2\bmod4$.  It carries exactly one
bit --- the finite-Fourier phase --- and both candidate readings sit on the same
side of it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The named open problem these objects are adjacent to}
\label{sec:adjacent}

Precisely: \emph{semilocal Weil positivity}, and within it, CCM's programme of
finding ``the semilocal analogue of the prolate operator''
(\cite{CCM2023} \file{mainc2m24fine.tex:197}--\file{:198}). \announced

The state of that programme, as of the principals' own most recent statements:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item Weil positivity is \emph{proved} at the archimedean place, for test

\item The semilocal \emph{trace formula} is Connes 1999 and is not the gap
(\file{rhready.tex:1233}--\file{:1238}). \proved
\item The semilocal \emph{Sonin space} is stable under enlarging $S$

\item The candidate semilocal prolate operator

(\file{:259}); a second candidate, via the Weil representation of the
metaplectic cover of $SL_2(\A_S)$, is deferred to the same (\file{:270}); and the

\subsection{The archimedean chain, and where the sign enters}

\cite{CC2021} proves: for $g\in C_c^\infty(\R_+^*)$ with support in
$[2^{-1/2},2^{1/2}]$ and $\widehat g(i/2)=\widehat g(0)=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CC2021}
  W_\infty(g\star g^*)\;\ge\;\operatorname{Tr}\big(\vartheta(g)\,\Sonin\,\vartheta(g)^*\big),
\end{equation}
$\Sonin$ the orthogonal projection onto the Sonin space. \proved{} Seventy pages
go into it.  The sign, however, enters in one line, and it enters twice in the
same way: $P\widehat PP\ge0$, and $\Sonin=\Sonin^*=\Sonin^2$, so
$\operatorname{Tr}(\vartheta(g)\Sonin\vartheta(g)^*)=\|\Sonin\vartheta(g)^*\|_{HS}^2\ge0$.
\emph{An involution and a square.}  The authors say so themselves in the
abstract (\file{weil-compo.tex:86}).  Everything else --- the operator
$Q=-(\rho\partial_\rho)^2+\tfrac14$ implementing the vanishing conditions
without changing supports, Boas--Kac, the trace-remainder $\delta$, its jump at
$\rho=1$, the dihedral decomposition, the Toeplitz analysis of $\mathcal K_I$
--- is sign-preserving bookkeeping and remainder control.

It is worth saying plainly what supplies the sign there, because it is
\emph{not} the prolate functions.  They supply the labels of the
two-dimensional pieces in the dihedral decomposition, i.e.\ the bookkeeping that
makes the remainder computable.  \textbf{Prolate theory is the coordinate
system, not the mechanism.}

The endgame turns on three numbers, all specific to $I=[\tfrac12,2]$:
$\mathcal K_I$ has exactly one eigenvalue $>1$, and only just
($\lambda_{\max}=1.05158$); the next is $\lambda_2=0.686494$, giving a gap
$>0.2278$ on the complement; and the offending eigenvector satisfies
$\langle\zeta\mid\xi_0\rangle\approx0.94865$, i.e.\ the bad direction is $95\%$
aligned with the one admissible linear condition.  Change any of the three
materially and the proof stops. \proved{} \cite{CC2021}

So any semilocal analogue must produce all three: a finite-dimensional bad
subspace, a spectral gap on its complement, and an alignment between each bad
direction and an admissible condition --- \emph{uniformly in $\lambda$}, which
the archimedean case never had to face, since it was proved at a single
$\lambda$.  Meanwhile the dimension of the near-radical grows like $2\mu$.

\subsection{What survives at a finite place, and what does not}

Most of the machinery survives.  The twist $\Delta^{1/2}$ and the involution
$f\mapsto f^*$, Boas--Kac, and the operator $Q$ are statements about
$C_c^\infty(\R_+^*)$, the same algebra at every place; the trace-formula
representation is proved semilocally (Connes 1999); the radical of $\QW$
containing $\operatorname{ran}\E$ is a statement about the semilocal form
already; and the semilocal Sonin space exists and is stable.  Positivity of
$\operatorname{Tr}(\vartheta(g)\Sonin_S\vartheta(g)^*)$ survives trivially,
because it is positivity of a compression. \proved

Two things do not survive, and they are the two that carry the sign.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{There is no semilocal Slepian--Pollak.}  The archimedean argument
needs the angle operator to be diagonalised by an explicitly known family with a
known spectrum, and a differential operator commuting with it --- what Connes'
survey calls ``the miraculous existence, discovered by the Bell Labs group''
(\file{rhready.tex:1293}).  Semilocally $P^S_T$ and $\widehat P^S_W$ exist and
the trace formula holds; the spectrum of $P^S\widehat P^SP^S$ is \emph{not
known}, and producing the operator that would make it computable is the entire
content of the semilocal-prolate-operator programme. \announced

\item \textbf{There is no comparison term at all.}  No semilocal $\delta$, no
semilocal $\epsilon$, no compactness statement, no spectral analysis.  The 60
pages that were the work at the archimedean place have not been begun
semilocally.
\end{enumerate}

In one sentence: \emph{the archimedean proof buys its sign with a compression and
pays for it with a diagonalisation; semilocally the compression is free and the
diagonalisation does not exist.}

\subsection{The prime terms are exactly indefinite}
\label{sec:indefinite}

This is a small theorem and it excludes a mechanism, so it is stated as one.
Put $x=\log u$, $L=\log\mu$, $a=\log n$; then $V(n)$ of \eqref{eq:hilbert} is
the compression to $L^2([-\tfrac L2,\tfrac L2])$ of $n^{-1/2}(T_a+T_a^*)$, with
$T_a$ translation by $a$.

\begin{proposition}[\ours, elementary]\label{prop:indefinite}
$V(n)$ is unitarily equivalent to $-V(n)$.  Its spectrum is exactly symmetric
about $0$, at every $\lambda$, before any support or vanishing condition is
imposed, and
\[
  \|V(n)\|=2n^{-1/2}\cos\!\Big(\frac{\pi}{m+2}\Big),
  \qquad m=\lceil L/\log n\rceil-1 .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Multiplication by $e^{i\pi x/a}$ is a unitary of $L^2([-\tfrac L2,\tfrac L2])$
and conjugates $T_a$ into $-T_a$, hence $V(n)$ into $-V(n)$.  The norm is the
Boas--Kac bound already used in \cite{CC2021} at \file{weil-compo.tex:842}.
\end{proof}

Verified numerically as well: spectrum symmetric to $10^{-15}$, and the closed
form matched at every row, on the full space and on the even sector that carries
$\sigma^+$ (\file{verify_semilocal_gap.py}, check 1; re-run on this pass).

Three consequences and one warning.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{No compression of a projection can produce the prime terms.}  A
compression $\vartheta(g)\Pi\vartheta(g)^*$ is positive; $V(n)$ is symmetric
about zero.  The mechanism of \eqref{eq:CC2021} does not extend to them even in
principle.
\item \textbf{The prime terms do not weaken as $\lambda$ grows.}  $\|V(n)\|$
increases with $L$ towards $2n^{-1/2}$.  Adding places does not perturb a proved
inequality; it adds a full-strength indefinite term.
\item \textbf{The number of them grows} like $\pi(\mu)+O(\sqrt\mu)$.
\item \textbf{Warning, and it is the house rule applied to this very
proposition.}  ``Exactly indefinite'' is \emph{invariant} under
$W\mapsto-W$.  It says a sign cannot come from here; it says nothing about which
sign.  It is admissible as a negative result and must not be quoted as though it
explained the direction of an inequality.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{One finite place is degenerate; the difficulty is between places}
\label{sec:burnol}

It is natural to ask whether the missing spectral description exists at a single
finite place.  It does, it has since 1999, and its content is that the object
degenerates.

Burnol \cite{Burnol1999} takes \emph{Connes' own operator}
$\widetilde{P_\Lambda}P_\Lambda U(f)$ over a non-archimedean field, sets
$\Lambda=q^n$, $Q_n:=\widetilde{P_n}P_n$, and decomposes by the characters of
the unit group.  His Theorem~XI gives the kernel of $Q_n^\chi$ in closed form in
every case; what matters here is what the proof says about the two projections
separately.  For ramified $\chi$ they are projections onto coordinate subspaces
of the \emph{same} orthonormal basis.  For trivial $\chi$ with $2n\ge\delta$, in
Burnol's own words, ``$P_n$ cuts off $\mathcal M_n$, $\widetilde{P_n}$ cuts off
$\mathcal L_n$, \emph{so they commute}''.  The remaining case $0\le2n<\delta$ is
empty over $\Q_p$, where the different exponent $\delta$ is $0$. \proved

\begin{observation}[\ours; Burnol does not phrase it this way]
Over $\Q_p$ the two cutoff projections commute at every $\Lambda=p^n$.  A
commuting pair has $P\widehat PP=P\widehat P$, a projection, with spectrum in
$\{0,1\}$; Connes' angle $\Theta$ takes only the values $0$ and $\pi/2$.  There
are no non-trivial angles, no plunge region, and hence no prolate family to find.
\end{observation}

This does not transfer to the semilocal case, and the reason is definitional, so
it can be checked without doing any mathematics.  The semilocal projections are
defined ``using the module'' $\mathrm{Mod}_S(u)=\prod_{v\in S}|u|_v$
(\file{rhready.tex:1236}--\file{:1240}), so $P^S_T$ cuts off
$\{x:\prod_v|x_v|_v\le T\}$ --- one condition on a \emph{product}, not a
condition at each place.  Hence $P^S_T\neq\bigotimes_v P_{T_v}$, and CCM state
only an \emph{inclusion} $\eta_S(P_\lambda)\subset P^S_\lambda$
(\file{mainc2m24fine.tex:766}--\file{:767}).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Consequence, stated flatly.}  Whatever difficulty the semilocal angle
operator has is located \emph{entirely in the coupling between places through
the product module}, not in any individual place.  Each finite place on its own
contributes a commuting pair and a one-dimensional Sonin space (CCM,
Prop.\ \texttt{soninppp}).
\end{quote}

It is worth recording that Connes 1999 substitutes a construction for a spectrum
at exactly this step: having given the archimedean plunge description in full,
he writes that for an arbitrary set of places ``we just use the same rule as in
the case of function fields'', i.e.\ the map $\psi\mapsto\psi\otimes1_R$
(\file{zeta.tex:2823}--\file{:2830}).  That map is CCM's $\eta_S$, which is
hilbertian and \emph{not} unitary.  This is the sharpest available statement of
the gap, and it is in Connes' own text.

Neither CCM nor the survey cites Burnol's p-adic scattering paper --- their
Burnol citations are the three archimedean/de~Branges papers --- which is why an
earlier absence-finding of ours missed it.  The one-place computation is outside
the corpus's citation graph.

\subsection{Status: observed, not proved}
\label{sec:notheorem}

Connes--Consani report that the archimedean contribution alone ceases to be
positive past $L=\log2$, that positivity is restored by adding the prime $2$,
and that perturbing $2$ to $1.9999$ or $2.0005$ destroys it
(\file{Spectraltriples.tex:542}--\file{:600}).  Their own prose is unambiguous
about the register: ``The first fact we \emph{report from the numerical
computations}\ldots'' (\file{:543}); ``we \emph{test numerically}\ldots''
(\file{:177}); ``we \emph{report graphical evidence}'' (\file{:210}).  The verbs
are indicative --- they assert the fact --- but they never prove it, and there is
\emph{no theorem, proposition, lemma or corollary environment anywhere} in the
three sensitivity subsections, checked mechanically. \observed

Where else this was looked for, so the negative can be told from an unexamined
one: \cite{CC2021} never asserts sharpness of its Theorem~1, and two nearby
statements are easy to mistake for it --- the proved remark that $D\circ Q$ is
not negative on $[2^{-1},2]$ (a different object: the remainder, limit

``no longer holds true when the interval length gets closer to $\log2$'' (the
\emph{method} degrading, not positivity failing).  Its own reason for the
interval cuts the other way: the interval is chosen ``so that rational primes are
not involved'', i.e.\ for convenience.  \cite{CCM2023} does not mention the
figures.  Connes' 2026 survey restates Theorem~1 twice with the same hypotheses
and never mentions that the archimedean form goes negative past that range ---
if sharpness were known as a theorem, that is where it would appear.  The one
place it could be hiding is Yoshida \cite{Yoshida1992}, which we did not read.

negative ones, of size $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-5}$.

\subsection{A witness, and why failure had to come eventually}
\label{sec:witness}

Two things the reproduction does not by itself give: a statement independent of
any matrix, and a reason.

\begin{proposition}[\ours]\label{prop:witness}
Let $f(x)=\cos(\pi x/L)+\kappa\cos(3\pi x/L)$ on $[-L/2,L/2]$ in the additive
coordinate $x=\log u$, continuous on $\R$ since both modes vanish at the
endpoints, with $\kappa$ fixed by $\int f(x)\cosh(x/2)\,dx=0$.  For real even $f$
that integral is $\widehat f(\pm i/2)$, so the boundary term of
\eqref{eq:hilbert} vanishes exactly and the archimedean value is $-W_\R$ alone.
It crosses zero at $\mu=3.5581$ and is negative at every larger $\mu$ tested;
at $\mu=4.0552$ it is $-0.646$ against $\|f\|^2=6.48$.
\end{proposition}

Evaluating it is three one-dimensional quadratures.  The threshold is weaker
than the true $2.2710$ --- it is one fixed vector, not the bottom eigenvector ---
but no truncation and no eigenvalue enters the argument, and the function can be
written down in a line.

\paragraph{The reason.}  On the Fourier side the archimedean part is
$\int|\widehat f(t)|^2\,\frac{2\vartheta'(t)}{2\pi}dt$ plus the boundary term,
and $\vartheta'$ is \emph{negative} on $|t|<6.2898$
($\vartheta'(0)=-2.68609170961\ldots$; first zero $6.2898360$).  So the Weil
density is negative in a neighbourhood of $t=0$, and the only thing stopping a
test function from living there is the uncertainty principle: a short support
forces $\widehat f$ to spread past the crossing, where $\vartheta'>0$.
Lengthening the support removes that obstruction.

This is a \emph{mechanism, not a proof} --- it says failure at large $L$ is
structurally expected and says nothing about where.  But it settles the shape of
the answer: the archimedean form is not a positive form that happens to be
verified on a small interval; it is an indefinite form whose positivity on a
small interval is an uncertainty-principle effect.  Turning it into a theorem ---
an explicit family with an explicit threshold --- looks routine and is not
attempted here.  \textbf{Gap G1} (\S\ref{sec:gaps}).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Consequence for the shape of the missing theorem.}  Neither the
archimedean term beyond $\mu=2.2710$ nor any $-\Lambda(n)V(n)$ at any $\mu$ is a
positive form.  Only the sum is --- conjecturally, since that is RH.  So the
required theorem is about \emph{cancellation between places}, not about the
assembly of per-place positives.  That is a different and harder object than the
one that was proved.
\end{quote}

\section{The archimedean deficit is bounded, and the balance is elsewhere}
\label{sec:deficit}
% ==========================================================================

Past the threshold, how does the archimedean deficit compare with what the
primes supply?  Write
\[
  \sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}=W_{0,2}-W_\R,\qquad
  D(\mu)=-\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}),\qquad
  R(\mu)=-\sum_pW_p(v_{\rm arch}),
\]
with $v_{\rm arch}$ the minimiser of $\sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}$; so $D$ is the
deficit and $R$ is what the primes supply \emph{on the direction where the
archimedean side is worst}.  All computations in this section are in arbitrary
precision, which is not caution: the quantity that settles the competition is
$\sim10^{-48}$ at $\mu=11$ against matrix entries of order $1$, so double
precision cannot represent the answer, let alone resolve it.

\subsection{The bound}
\label{sec:bound}

\begin{theorem}[\ours, \proved{} given \cite{CC2023} Prop.\ \texttt{Hilbert}]
\label{thm:deficit}
For every $\mu$ and every unit $f$ in the even sector,
$\sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}(f,f)\ge2\vartheta'(0)\|f\|^2$; hence
\[
  D(\mu)\;<\;-2\vartheta'(0)\;=\;\log\pi-\psi(\tfrac14)\;=\;5.3721834192\ldots
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Three one-line facts applied to \eqref{eq:hilbert}.
(i) \emph{$\vartheta'$ attains its minimum at $t=0$.}
$\vartheta'(t)=\tfrac12\Re\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2)-\tfrac12\log\pi$ and
$\frac{d}{dt}\Re\psi(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2)=-\tfrac12\Im\psi'(\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2)$;
since $\psi'(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}(z+k)^{-2}$, at $z=\tfrac14+\tfrac{it}2$ each term
has imaginary part $-t(k+\tfrac14)/|z+k|^4<0$ for $t>0$.  So $\Im\psi'<0$,
$\vartheta'$ is strictly increasing on $t>0$, and
$\inf_t\vartheta'=\vartheta'(0)$.
(ii) \emph{On the even sector the boundary term is a square}: for real $f$ even
under $u\mapsto u^{-1}$, $\widehat f(\tfrac i2)=\widehat f(-\tfrac i2)=\int
F\cosh(x/2)dx$ is real, so the term is $2(\int F\cosh(x/2)dx)^2\ge0$.
(iii) \emph{Plancherel} in this normalisation:
$\int|\widehat f(t)|^2dt/2\pi=\|f\|^2$.
\end{proof}

What is not re-proved is Proposition \texttt{Hilbert} itself, which is proved in
\cite{CC2023}.  Its normalisation is the one place this could silently go wrong
by a factor of $2$, so it is \emph{checked and not assumed}: both sides of
\eqref{eq:hilbert} were evaluated on $f=\xi_1$, where $\widehat f$ is
closed-form, agreeing to $2\times10^{-8}$ at $\mu=2$ and $1\times10^{-8}$ at
$\mu=20$ (the residual is the $\log t/t^2$ tail, added from its asymptotic
form), with Plancherel confirmed at $0.999999998$.

That check is limited by its own test function: $\widehat{\xi_1}$ decays like
$t^{-1}$, so $|\widehat f|^2\vartheta'$ decays like $\log t/t^2$ and the tail has
to be supplied rather than measured.  The recheck of \S\ref{sec:recheck} removes
the difficulty by changing the test function to
$f=(1+\cos(a_1x))^2=\tfrac32+2\cos(a_1x)+\tfrac12\cos(a_2x)$, which vanishes to
second order at $\pm L/2$; its transform has both the $t^{-1}$ and the $t^{-3}$
coefficients cancelling identically, leaving
$\widehat f\sim12a_1^4\sin(tL/2)/t^5$, so the tail past a cutoff $T$ is
$O(\log T/T^9)$ and can simply be dropped.  At $\mu=8$, cutting off at $T=200$,
\[
  \Big(\int|\widehat f(t)|^2\tfrac{2\vartheta'(t)}{2\pi}\,dt\Big)
  \Big/\big(-W_\R(f,f)\big)\;=\;1.000000000000000007 ,
\]
and the residual is the tail rather than a constant: the same cutoff applied to
Plancherel --- which is exact in this normalisation --- leaves a residual of the
same size, raising the working precision from $40$ to $70$ digits moves neither,
and raising $T$ from $60$ to $200$ improves the ratio by a factor
$3.1\times10^4$ against the
$(200/60)^9=2.6\times10^4$ that $O(T^{-9})$ predicts.  \emph{There is no extra
factor of $2$, to $18$ digits.} \observed{} \ours{} (\S\ref{sec:recheck})

What remains taken on trust is the proposition itself, not its normalisation.
Their own proof of it says $\vartheta'$ is ``lower bounded'' and never extracts
the constant.

\section{The scale of \texorpdfstring{$s(\mu)$}{s(mu)}}

\subsection{The index is forced, and it is 4}
\label{sec:index}

$\E$ is defined on $\mathcal S_0^{ev}$, i.e.\ $f(0)=\widehat f(0)=0$.  These two
conditions are not optional: they are what makes $\E f$ a test function at all.
For $f=\sum_mb_m\psi_{m,\lambda}$,
\[
  f(0)=\sum_mb_m\psi_m(0),\qquad
  \widehat f(0)=\int f=\sum_mb_m\chi_m\psi_m(0),
\]
the second because $\F_{e_\R}\psi_m=\chi_m\psi_m$ on $[-\lambda,\lambda]$
evaluated at $0$.  Two modes can satisfy both \emph{only if
$\chi_{m_1}=\chi_{m_2}$}; otherwise the $2\times2$ system is non-singular and
forces $b=0$.  Since $\chi_m\simeq(-1)^m$, that requires $m_1\equiv m_2\bmod2$
in Connes--Consani's labelling, i.e.\ \emph{prolate indices congruent mod 4}.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item $m=0$ alone: $\psi_0(0)\ne0$, inadmissible.
\item prolate indices $\{0,2\}$: $\chi\simeq+1$ and $-1$.  Excluded \emph{by the
phase, not by size}.
\item prolate indices $\{0,4\}$: both $\chi\simeq+1$.  Admissible, and minimal.
This is Connes--Consani's $\phi_2$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent So the governing defect is $1-\Lambda_4$, i.e.\ $1-\chi_2$ in Connes'
notation. \proved{} (\ours{} as an argument; the construction is
Connes--Consani's, and mode-4 selection by the finite-Fourier phase was found
earlier in this project in the Hermite rather than the prolate limit.)

\begin{remark}[a correction to Connes--Consani's construction, and it is free]
\label{rem:thirdmode}
Since $\chi_0=\chi_2$ only approximately, $\phi_2$ satisfies $\phi_2(0)=0$
exactly but
$\widehat{\phi_2}(0)=(\chi_0-\chi_2)\psi_0(0)\psi_2(0)\neq0$, with
$|\chi_0-\chi_2|\simeq1-\chi_2$.  That is not a rounding remark: by Poisson a
non-zero $\widehat f(0)$ puts an $x^{-1/2}$ tail on $\E f$ as $x\to0$, which is
not in $L^2(d^*u)$, and equivalently a pole in $\F_\mu(\E f)$ at $s=\pm\tfrac i2$
--- which is exactly what the two conditions are for.  It can be removed exactly
with a third mode: taking $\phi=b_0\psi_0+b_2\psi_2+b_4\psi_4$ (prolate indices
$0,4,8$, all with $\chi\simeq+1$) and imposing both conditions gives
$u_4/u_2\simeq-(1-\chi_2)/(1-\chi_4)$, a relative weight of $1.3\times10^{-8}$ at
$\mu=12$.  The vector is still essentially $\phi_2$ and the governing defect is
still $1-\Lambda_4$. \ours
\end{remark}

\subsection{Fuchs at general index, and Connes' constant}
\label{sec:fuchs}

Fuchs' asymptotic, in the form Connes cites it, is
$1-\Lambda_n(c)\sim4\sqrt\pi\,8^nc^{\,n+1/2}e^{-2c}/n!$.  Connes states
(\file{rhready.tex:1149})
\[
  1-\chi_2\;\sim\;\frac{2^{14}\sqrt2\,\pi^5}{3}\,\mu^{9/2}e^{-4\pi\mu},
\]
and with $1-\chi_2=\tfrac12(1-\Lambda_4)+O((1-\Lambda_4)^2)$ and $c=2\pi\mu$
this is \emph{identically} one half of Fuchs at $n=4$ --- verified to $100$
digits, difference $1.4\times10^{-101}$.  The $9/2$ is $n+\tfrac12$ at $n=4$.
\proved{} \theirs{Connes, from Fuchs}

Two things follow that matter for what any numerical agreement can be evidence
\emph{for}.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The rate cannot discriminate the index.}  The exponential factor
$e^{-2c}$ in Fuchs \emph{does not depend on $n$}.  At $c=2\pi\mu$ every prolate
index predicts the same rate $2c/\mu=4\pi$.  A test that every candidate passes
is not a test.  What discriminates is the power $n+\tfrac12$ and the constant ---
and both are published. \ours{} as an observation
\end{quote}

\noindent Also, the asymptotic is not accurate enough to be used as the
comparison object.  Checked at $260$ digits against exact prolate eigenvalues,
the ratio asymptotic/exact at $n=4$ is $1.31219$ at $c=2\pi\cdot5$, $1.17936$ at
$c=2\pi\cdot8$, $1.11442$ at $c=2\pi\cdot12$, falling to $1.02897$ at $c=280$:
Fuchs \emph{over}-estimates by $1+O(1/c)$, and by $11$--$31\%$ over the whole
range where $s(\mu)$ is computed.  So the confrontation below uses exact
$1-\chi_2$.  (This is a check of the formula against the thing it approximates;
an earlier pass of ours had checked two \emph{formulas} against each other,
which is a weaker statement.) \ours

\subsection{The truncation bias, measured rather than asserted}
\label{sec:truncbias}

Truncation is variational and cuts one way, so every $s$ above is an
\emph{upper} bound, looser at larger $\mu$ --- which is the direction that
inflates $s/(1-\chi_2)$ at large $\mu$.  Computing $s(\mu)$ at $N=60,80,100,120$
and extrapolating geometrically in $N$ leaves ratios
$7.11,\ 9.65,\ 8.71,\ 9.92,\ 8.21,\ 9.21$ at $\mu=5,7,8,10,11,12$ --- a factor
$1.40$ spread with \emph{no monotone trend left in it}.  (At $\mu=6$ and $\mu=9$
the last two increments are not decreasing, so the geometric fit refuses and no
extrapolation is reported: that is the fit declining, not the sequence
diverging.)  At $N=100$ the ratio rose by $1.45$ across the range; after
extrapolation the rise is gone.

The extrapolation is a three-point model, not a theorem, but it can only move
the ratio \emph{down}, and it moves it down more at large $\mu$.  The claim made
here is therefore only the weak one: \emph{$\kappa=s/(1-\chi_2)$ grows no faster
than $\log\mu$}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:q3} now proves the corresponding
\emph{upper} statement outright, at $\Xi=O(\mu^6\log^3\!\mu)$, which is
polynomial rather than logarithmic and so is compatible with the measurement
without confirming it --- the logarithm being what the mean zero density
$\frac1{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}$ suggests and what the truncation of
\S\ref{sec:q3} pays $\mu^3$ to avoid needing.  Either
way it is sub-polynomial in the exponent and cannot touch an exponential rate.  Whether $\kappa$
is a constant of about $9$ is \textbf{Gap G4}.

\label{sec:theirnumber}

\subsection{The identity, and the one inequality that is missing}
\label{sec:identity}

Everything in Connes--Consani's chain is exact except the last step, and the
last step is where a proof would live.  Here is what the chain actually gives.

Let $\phi$ be the normalised three-mode vector of Remark~\ref{rem:thirdmode}, so
that $\E\phi\in L^2(d^*u)$ and $\F_\mu(\E\phi)$ has no pole at $s=\pm\tfrac i2$.
Since $\operatorname{supp}\phi\subseteq[-\lambda,\lambda]$ we have
$\operatorname{supp}\E\phi\subseteq(0,\lambda]$, so with
\[
  g:=\E(\phi)\big|_{[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]},\qquad
  r:=\E(\phi)\big|_{(0,\lambda^{-1})},\qquad \E(\phi)=g+r,
\]
the decomposition is exhaustive: nothing spills above $\lambda$.

\begin{proposition}[\ours, from \cite{CC2023}]\label{prop:identity}
$\displaystyle \QW_\lambda(g,g)=\sum_{\frac12+is\in Z}\big|\F_\mu r(s)\big|^2 .$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\F_\mu(\E\phi)$ vanishes on $Z$, so $\F_\mu g=-\F_\mu r$ on $Z$.
\end{proof}

This is an identity and it is the conceptual content: \textbf{$\QW_\lambda$
evaluated at the prolate vector is the Weil form of the part of $\E\phi$ that
falls outside the window.}  The near-radical is not ``nearly in the kernel'' by
accident; it is exactly as far from the kernel as the construction fails to fit
inside $[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$.

\paragraph{The size of the spill.}  By Poisson, for $x<\lambda^{-1}$,
$\E\phi(x)=\E\widehat\phi(x^{-1})$, so with $y=x^{-1}>\lambda$,
$\|r\|^2=\int_\lambda^\infty|\sum_{n>0}\widehat\phi(ny)|^2dy$.  The $n=1$
diagonal term is half the out-of-band energy of $\phi$, and that energy splits
\emph{exactly} across the prolate modes, since
$\langle\widehat{\mathcal P_\lambda}\psi_n,\widehat{\mathcal P_\lambda}\psi_m\rangle=\Lambda_m\delta_{nm}$
makes the in-band parts orthogonal and hence the out-of-band parts too:
\[
  \big\|(1-\widehat{\mathcal P_\lambda})\phi\big\|^2
  =\sum_m|b_m|^2(1-\Lambda_{2m})=w\,(1-\Lambda_4)(1+o(1)),\qquad w\to\tfrac8{11}.
\]
So $\|r\|^2\asymp(1-\chi_2)$, linearly, and the corpus's central estimate
$\QW_\lambda(\E h_\lambda)\asymp1-\chi_4$ is the $n=1$ term of this line, with
the constant it hides being $\tfrac8{11}$ times an $O(1)$ mean value over the
zeros.  \emph{The $n\ge2$ terms and the cross terms are not free}: $\widehat\phi$
decays only like $y^{-1}$ off-band and the Cauchy--Schwarz sum is
logarithmically divergent before the oscillation of $\widehat\phi$ is used.  The
honest form would be $\|r\|^2=C_1(\lambda)(1-\chi_2)$ with $C_1=O(\log\mu)$;
that was listed as a gap in the first draft of this paper and is now a
consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:q2}, which is exactly the statement that the
oscillation of $\widehat\phi$ does the work Cauchy--Schwarz will not do
(\S\ref{sec:q2}).

\paragraph{The two hypotheses this once needed.}  Converting the identity into a
bound on $s$ needs a lower bound on $\|g\|^2$ and an upper bound on the sum over
zeros.  They are recorded here in the form the first draft of this paper gave
them, because \S\ref{sec:unconditional} disposes of them in ways that make the
exact wording matter:

\setcounter{hypothesis}{-1}
\begin{hypothesis}\label{H0}
$\|g\|^2$ is bounded below, uniformly in $\lambda$.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}\label{H1}
There is $\Theta(\lambda)$, subexponential in $\mu$, with
$\sum_{\frac12+is\in Z}|\F_\mu r(s)|^2\le\Theta(\lambda)\|r\|^2$ for the spill
$r$.
\end{hypothesis}

\noindent The first draft glossed \eqref{H0} as ``a mean-value statement about
$|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^2$ against an explicit weight'' and \eqref{H1} as ``a
mean-value estimate for the zeta zeros as a sampling set, of
Plancherel--P\'olya type'', and listed the pair as the one genuinely open
analytic problem the line produces.  \emph{Both glosses were wrong, and not in
the same way.}  There is no $\zeta$ in \eqref{H0} at all
(\S\ref{sec:h0}); and \eqref{H1} \emph{as written} is not open but is not a
possible target of proof either, so what replaces it is a different estimate
(\S\ref{sec:notH1}).  Both are disposed of in \S\ref{sec:unconditional}, and the
conclusion below is unconditional.  The correct one-line description of what
\eqref{H1} was reaching for, replacing ``of Plancherel--P\'olya type'', is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{a weighted-$L^2$ tail bound for the entire extension of a prolate function
outside its interval.}
\end{quote}

\noindent That names a problem with no number theory in it, about a classical
special function studied since 1961 and computable to arbitrary precision ---
none of which was true of \eqref{H1} as stated, and all of which is why the
chain closed.

\begin{corollary}[machine-checked]\label{cor:upper}
Suppose \eqref{H0} holds, suppose
$\sum_{\frac12+is\in Z}|\F_\mu r(s)|^2\le\Xi(\mu)(1-\chi_2(\lambda))$ with $\Xi$
subexponential in $\mu$, and suppose
\textbf{(F)} $\mu^{-1}\log\big(1-\chi_2(\lambda)\big)\to-4\pi$.  Then
\[
  s(\mu)\;\le\;\frac{\QW_\lambda(g,g)}{\|g\|^2}\;\le\;
  \frac{\Xi(\mu)}{\|g\|^2}\,(1-\chi_2(\lambda)),
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  \limsup_{\mu\to\infty}\frac{\log s(\mu)}{\mu}\;\le\;-4\pi .
\]
\end{corollary}

The three hypotheses are supplied as follows: the second by
Theorem~\ref{thm:q3}, the first by Theorem~\ref{thm:h0}, and \textbf{(F)} by
Fuchs' asymptotic in the form Connes cites it (\S\ref{sec:fuchs}).  Note what the
corollary actually consumes of \eqref{H0}, because it is weaker than the
hypothesis and the difference is what made \eqref{H0} tractable: only
$\mu^{-1}\log\|g\|^2\to0$, so \emph{any} $\|g\|^2\ge e^{-o(\mu)}$ gives the same
$-4\pi$.

\paragraph{Hypothesis (F) was not in the printed statement, and it should have
been --- because it is where the $4\pi$ comes from.}  An earlier form of this
corollary assumed \eqref{H0} and the $\Xi$ bound only, and concluded the
$-4\pi$.  It does not follow from those two: $\Xi$ and $\|g\|^2$ are both
subexponential, so \emph{nothing in them mentions $4\pi$ at all}, and the rate
has to enter through $1-\chi_2$.  \emph{The omission was found by formalising the
corollary}: \file{lean/Riemann/Assembly.lean}'s
\texttt{limsup\_le\_neg\_four\_pi} cannot be stated without its \texttt{hN}
hypothesis, which is (F).  Two consequences worth stating plainly.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The $4\pi$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} enters through Fuchs and only
through Fuchs.}  It is not produced anywhere in \S\ref{sec:unconditional}; every
theorem there is a bound by a subexponential factor times $1-\chi_2$.  This is
consistent with \S\ref{sec:fuchs}'s own observation that the rate cannot
discriminate the index --- the rate is not ours to discriminate with.
\item \textbf{So Theorem~\ref{thm:main}'s external ledger has two entries, not
one.}  Its status line reads ``\proved{} modulo \cite{Dunster2017} eq.\
(124)+(107)'' and now also names \cite{Fuchs1964}, which
\S\ref{sec:provenance} lists under ``not read''.  \emph{Nothing about ``unconditional'' changes} ---
Fuchs' asymptotic is classical and \proved{} --- but a paper that counts citation
depth to two decimal places elsewhere (\textbf{G13}) was one citation short here.
Recorded in \S\ref{sec:corrections}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{And the corollary is machine-checked.}  Given its three hypotheses,
the passage to $\limsup\mu^{-1}\log s(\mu)\le-4\pi$ is formalised in Lean~4
(\S\ref{sec:standards}), together with an instantiation at this paper's own
exponents --- $\Xi=C\mu^6\log^3\!\mu$, $1-\chi_2=C'\mu^{9/2}e^{-4\pi\mu}$,
$\|g\|^2\to0.219\ldots$ --- so that the hypotheses are exhibited as satisfiable
rather than merely assumed.  The primary formalised statement is the $\epsilon$
form, \emph{for every $\epsilon>0$, eventually $\log s(\mu)\le(-4\pi+\epsilon)\mu$},
which is what the chain gives and is stronger than the $\limsup$; the $\limsup$
is to be read in $[-\infty,\infty]$ as usual.  \emph{What is checked is the
skeleton, not the legs}: the four legs are hypotheses, and two of them are
prose.

That is a real half of the answer, and it is the half every number in this
project is consistent with, because every computed $s$ is itself an upper bound.
It also says that the residual truncation bias and the identification's
one-sidedness are the \emph{same} fact and not two.

\subsection{What is proved is (4.3), not \texorpdfstring{\eqref{H1}}{H1} as
written}
\label{sec:notH1}

This subsection comes first because the convenient sentence --- ``\eqref{H1} is
proved'' --- is available, is what a reader will write down, and is not what
happened.  What is proved is the estimate of Theorem~\ref{thm:q3}, which bounds
the same sum against $(1-\chi_2)$ rather than against $\|r\|^2$.  The difference
is not bookkeeping, and this subsection is why.

\begin{proposition}[\ours]\label{prop:noH1}
\emph{(i)} For each fixed $\lambda$, \eqref{H1} holds trivially, with
$\Theta(\lambda):=\sum_\rho|\F_\mu r(s_\rho)|^2/\|r\|^2$; the sum converges by
\S\ref{sec:zetaside}.  So the entire content of \eqref{H1} is the growth of that
one ratio in $\lambda$.  \emph{(ii)} No proof of it can use $\|r\|^2$ alone:
over $L^2$ no uniform $\Theta$ exists, because $\F_\mu r$ is Hardy-class on a
half-plane whose boundary carries the sampling points, and point evaluation
there is an unbounded functional.
\end{proposition}

Both halves matter, and the second is the reason the first is not a triviality
one can quote past.

\paragraph{It is a ratio, not a hypothesis.}  For each $\lambda$ there is exactly
one $r$, so there is no class of functions for a general theorem to be applied
to.  \eqref{H1} therefore cannot be ``reduced to a known theorem'' in the
abstract at all: any proof has to be applied to \emph{this} $r$, and then what
is used is whatever structure this $r$ has --- which is exactly the structure
\S\S\ref{sec:lemma51}--\ref{sec:q2} supply, and which $\|r\|$ does not see.

\paragraph{And the normalisation by $\|r\|^2$ is the wrong denominator.}
Substituting
$u=e^{-v}$ and $R(v)=r(e^{-v})$, supported on $[\log\lambda,\infty)$,
\[
  \F_\mu r(s)=\int_{\log\lambda}^\infty R(v)e^{isv}dv ,
\]
which is (up to a unimodular factor) a Hardy-space $H^2(\C^+)$ function, with
$\|\F_\mu r\|^2_{L^2(\R)}=2\pi\|r\|^2$.  Point evaluation of an $H^2$ function
at a \emph{boundary} point is an unbounded functional.  Under RH every $s_\rho$
is real, i.e.\ every sampling point is on that boundary.  So
$\sum_\rho|\F_\mu r(s_\rho)|^2\le\Theta\|r\|^2$ holds for \emph{no} finite
$\Theta$ uniformly over $L^2$, at any $\lambda$ --- and hence any proof of it
for our $r$ must use decay or smoothness that $\|r\|$ does not see, at which
point $\|r\|^2$ has stopped being the object doing the work.  (This is also
visible from the form itself: $\QW_\lambda$ takes values in $(-\infty,+\infty]$
and is unbounded above.)  \proved{} \ours

\paragraph{Two named theorems that cannot reach it, and why that is structural.}
The first draft called \eqref{H1} a bound ``of Plancherel--P\'olya type''.
Plancherel--P\'olya requires (i) $f$ entire of exponential type --- equivalently
$\widecheck f$ compactly supported --- and (ii) uniformly separated sampling
nodes.  \emph{Both fail, and the first fails structurally}: $R$ is supported on
a half-line precisely \emph{because} $r$ is a spill, so the very fact that
creates the near-radical vector destroys the Paley--Wiener hypothesis, and no
repair of the argument restores it.  And the zeros are not separated: the mean
gap $2\pi/\log(\gamma/2\pi)\to0$, and the relatively-separated version needs a
bounded $N$ where ours is $\asymp\log T$.  The Hardy-space replacement is worse:
Carleson-measure theorems bound $\sum_k|F(z_k)|^2\Im z_k$, and \emph{under RH
that weight vanishes identically}.  \proved{} \ours

\begin{quote}
\textbf{So the correct sentence is this one.}  \emph{The upper bound
$\limsup\mu^{-1}\log s(\mu)\le-4\pi$ is unconditional.  Hypothesis \eqref{H1} as
stated is bypassed rather than proved --- its $\|r\|^2$ normalisation is ruled
out as a target of proof --- and is replaced by the estimate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:q3}, which is what Corollary~\ref{cor:upper} consumes.
Hypothesis \eqref{H0} is proved.}  Anyone quoting this paper should quote that,
and not ``H1 is proved''.
\end{quote}

Recorded so this subsection is not over-read in the other direction, since it
would be easy to make it say more than it does.  None of it asserts that
\eqref{H1} is \emph{false} for our family --- Proposition~\ref{prop:noH1}(i)
says the opposite at each fixed $\lambda$, and with the $\|r\|^2\asymp(1-\chi_2)$
of \S\ref{sec:identity} a subexponential $\Theta$ is in fact what one would
expect.  What is ruled out is the \emph{shape}: a hypothesis whose right-hand
side is $\|r\|^2$ names a quantity no proof can be built on, so it cannot be the
statement a chain of theorems terminates in.  And none of it is a connection
between \eqref{H1} and RH.  It is a statement about which proof techniques
apply, and its practical consequence is the one recorded above: quote (4.3).

\subsection{The zeta side discharges unconditionally}
\label{sec:zetaside}

This is a positive result and it belongs in the body rather than in a gap list:
\emph{the zeros are not the obstruction}.  Two classical unconditional inputs,
and nothing else about $\zeta$ is used anywhere in this section.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{(RvM)} $\#\{\rho:|\gamma_\rho-t|\le1\}\le C_1\log(3+|t|)$, with
multiplicity (Riemann--von Mangoldt). \proved{} \theirs{classical}
\item \textbf{(ZFR)} $\frac{c_0}{\log(3+|\gamma|)}\le\beta\le1-\frac{c_0}{\log(3+|\gamma|)}$
(de la Vall\'ee Poussin, with the functional equation for the left inequality).
\proved{} \theirs{classical}
\end{itemize}

\noindent Write $F:=\F_\mu r$ and, with $R(v):=r(e^{-v})$ supported on
$[\log\lambda,\infty)$, assume the decay
\textbf{(D)}: $|R(v)|\le Ae^{-v/2}$ there.  (D) makes $F$ holomorphic on the
half-plane $\{\Im s>-\tfrac12\}$, which contains the strip $|\Im s|<\tfrac12$
and hence every $s_\rho$ --- unconditionally, since $\Im s_\rho=\beta_\rho-\frac12$
and $0<\beta_\rho<1$.  \S\ref{sec:q2} shows (D) is exactly what the prolate
structure supplies.

\begin{proposition}[\ours]\label{prop:zetaside}
Under \textup{(D)},
\[
  \sum_\rho|F(s_\rho)|^2\;\le\;\frac{C}{c_0^2}\int_{-1/2}^{1/2}\!\!\int_\R
  \log^3(3+|t|)\,|F(t+i\sigma)|^2\,dt\,d\sigma ,
\]
and for each $|\sigma|<\tfrac12$, $\int_\R|F(t+i\sigma)|^2dt
=2\pi\int_{\log\lambda}^\infty|R(v)|^2e^{-2\sigma v}dv$ by Plancherel.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put $\epsilon_\rho:=\tfrac12\min(1,\beta_\rho,1-\beta_\rho)$, so
$\epsilon_\rho\ge c_0/(2\log(3+|\gamma_\rho|))$ by (ZFR) and the disc
$D(s_\rho,\epsilon_\rho)$ lies in the strip where $F$ is holomorphic.  $|F|^2$
is subharmonic, so
$|F(s_\rho)|^2\le(\pi\epsilon_\rho^2)^{-1}\iint_{D(s_\rho,\epsilon_\rho)}|F|^2dA$.
Sum over $\rho$: a point $t+i\sigma$ lies in $D(s_\rho,\epsilon_\rho)$ only if
$|\gamma_\rho-t|\le1$, so by (RvM) it is covered $\le C_1\log(3+|t|)$ times, and
each covering $\rho$ carries weight
$(\pi\epsilon_\rho^2)^{-1}\ll\log^2(3+|t|)/c_0^2$.
\end{proof}

\emph{After this there are no zeros left in the problem}, and what remains is a
weighted-$L^2$ tail bound for one explicit special function.  Two things worth
saying about this proposition rather than passing over it.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{It makes the logarithmic zero density the mechanism, not an
aside.}  The first draft observed that ``the sum converges only because the zero
density is logarithmic''.  That is right, and Proposition~\ref{prop:zetaside} is
where it is used: the density enters exactly twice, as the covering multiplicity
and --- through (ZFR) --- as the radius of the discs.  Nothing else about the
distribution of the zeros is needed, and in particular RH is not, either as a
hypothesis or as an obstacle.
\item \textbf{It absorbs two gaps into one.}  The first draft listed the bound
on $\|r\|^2$ (G5) and the bound on the zero sum (G6) separately.  They are not
independent: Proposition~\ref{prop:zetaside} bounds the zero sum by weighted
norms of the spill, and \S\ref{sec:q2} bounds those, so a single hypothesis
about a prolate function discharges both.  Note also that $\|r\|^2$ itself never
appears in the final estimate --- consistent with \S\ref{sec:notH1}, and the
reason G5 comes along for free.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The amplitude lemma}
\label{sec:lemma51}

Everything from here to \S\ref{sec:q3} is about the entire extension of a
prolate function outside its interval.  There is no number theory in any of it.
Write $\Phi=\Phi_{n,c}=\PS_{n,0}$ on $[-1,1]$ at bandwidth $c=2\pi\mu$,
normalised by $\int_{-1}^1\Phi^2=1$ and extended to the entire function it is;
$\chi_n$ is the eigenvalue of the prolate differential operator and $\Lambda_n$
the Slepian concentration eigenvalue.

The exact identity that makes the whole reduction possible is classical in
substance: the finite Fourier relation
$\int_{-1}^1\Phi_n(y)e^{icxy}dy=\mu_\Phi\Phi_n(x)$ holds for \emph{all} $x$, and
$\Lambda_n=\frac{c}{2\pi}\mu_\Phi^2$, so by Plancherel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outofband}
  \int_{|x|>1}|\Phi_n(x)|^2\,dx\;=\;\frac{1-\Lambda_n}{\Lambda_n}.
\end{equation}
\emph{The out-of-band energy of a prolate function is the integral of its own
entire extension outside the interval} --- so the small quantity $1-\Lambda_n$
and the pointwise size of $\Phi$ off the interval are the same object. \proved

\begin{lemma}[\ours, elementary; machine-checked]\label{lem:amplitude}
Let $\Phi$ satisfy $\big((1-x^2)\Phi'\big)'+(\chi_n-c^2x^2)\Phi=0$ on
$(1,\infty)$ and suppose $\chi_n<c^2$.  Suppose further that
\emph{(a)} $\Phi'$ is bounded on some right-neighbourhood of $1$, and
\emph{(b)} $\Phi$ is right-continuous at $1$.
Then $|\Phi(x)|\le|\Phi(1)|$ for all $x\ge1$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent \emph{(a) and (b) are two hypotheses and not one.}  Both hold for
$\Phi$ entire, which is the only case used here; they are separated because the
proof uses them at two different points and only one of them is what
``boundedness near $x=1$'' names.  The separation is not a stylistic
preference --- it is what formalising the lemma forced (\S\ref{sec:standards}),
and the half a reader skips is (b).

\begin{proof}
For $x>1$ put $p:=(x^2-1)\Phi'$, $q:=c^2x^2-\chi_n$ and $D:=(x^2-1)q$, so the
equation reads $p'=-q\Phi$.  $\chi_n<c^2$ gives $q>0$ on $[1,\infty)$ and
$D'=2x(2c^2x^2-\chi_n-c^2)>0$ there, since at $x=1$ the bracket is
$c^2-\chi_n>0$ and increases.  With $V:=p^2/D+\Phi^2$ and
$\Phi'=p/(x^2-1)$,
\[
  V'=\frac{2pp'}{D}-\frac{p^2D'}{D^2}+2\Phi\Phi'
   =-\frac{2pq\Phi}{D}-\frac{p^2D'}{D^2}+\frac{2\Phi p}{x^2-1}
   =-\,p^2\frac{D'}{D^2}\;\le\;0 ,
\]
the first and third terms cancelling identically because $D=(x^2-1)q$.  For the
endpoint value, $p^2/D=(x^2-1)(\Phi')^2/q$ with $q(1)=c^2-\chi_n>0$, so
\emph{(a)} alone gives $p^2/D\to0$ as $x\to1^+$; and $\Phi(x)^2\to\Phi(1)^2$ is
\emph{(b)}.  Together $V(1^+)=\Phi(1)^2$ --- and $p^2/D\to0$ by itself does not
give it.  Finally $\Phi^2\le V\le V(1^+)$.
\end{proof}

Two remarks, the second of which is why this lemma is in the body rather than
inside the next proof.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The hypothesis is the regime we are in, and it is the complement
of the published one.}  Measured, $\chi_n/c^2$ runs $0.020$ to $0.64$ over the
indices $n=0,2,4$ and bandwidths $c=2\pi\mu$, $\mu=2,\dots,8$; and
$\chi_n\sim(2n+1)c$ against $c^2$, so for each fixed $n$ the hypothesis holds
once $c$ is large.  The sharpest published work on prolates off the interval
\cite{OsipovRokhlin2013} assumes $\chi_n>c^2$ throughout --- the
regime of index \emph{above} the plunge --- so its theorems do not apply here,
and the one result of theirs that holds in every regime is an exact identity
whose remainder is the out-of-band leakage of $t\Phi(t)$, an object of the same
size as the quantity being bounded.  Hypotheses read in the source. \proved
\item \textbf{It is also the connection.}  Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude} supplies
the amplitude \emph{at a point away from the singular point} $x=1$, which is
what lets the asymptotic argument of \S\ref{sec:q1} start at $x=\sqrt2$ and
never cross $x=1$.  The first draft of the underlying note called the missing
step ``a Bessel-type connection problem at the regular singular point''; that
diagnosis was wrong, and this lemma is why.
\item \textbf{It is machine-checked, and that is where hypothesis (b) came
from.}  This lemma, and the coefficient bounds \S\ref{sec:q1} runs on, are
formalised in Lean~4 against mathlib (\S\ref{sec:standards}).  The informal
statement carried ``$\Phi$ analytic at $x=1$'' with the remark that
``boundedness near $x=1$ would do''; the remark names (a) and not (b), and
without (b) the endpoint value $V(1^+)=\Phi(1)^2$ does not follow.  \emph{The
informal proof was not wrong} --- analyticity supplies both --- but its stated
weakening was, and a machine found it on the first attempt at the first
lemma.  The formalisation also localises what each bound rests on: $\chi\ge0$
is used at exactly one step of \S\ref{sec:q1}, confirming by a second method
the hypothesis tightening from $\chi<c^2$ to $0\le\chi<c^2$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Q1: the single-term bound, with a bounded constant}
\label{sec:q1}

\begin{theorem}[\ours]\label{thm:q1}
Let $c>\sqrt2$ and $0\le\chi<c^2$, and let $\Phi\not\equiv0$ be a real solution
of $\big((1-x^2)\Phi'\big)'+(\chi-c^2x^2)\Phi=0$ analytic at $x=1$.  Then
$\Phi(1)\ne0$ and
\[
  x\,|\Phi(x)|\;\le\;K(c)\,|\Phi(1)|\qquad(x\ge1),\qquad
  K(c)=2^{3/4}e^{E(c)},
\]
\[
  E(c)=\frac{5\sqrt2}{c-\sqrt2}+\frac{\sqrt2\,c/3+2}{(c-\sqrt2)^2}.
\]
$K$ depends on $c$ alone --- in particular it is \emph{independent of $\chi$,
hence of the index $n$}, throughout $0\le\chi<c^2$ --- and $K(c)$ decreases to
$2^{3/4}=1.68179\ldots$ as $c\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

Sketch, because the mechanism is the interesting part.  On $1\le x\le\sqrt2$
there is nothing to prove: Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude} gives
$x|\Phi(x)|\le\sqrt2|\Phi(1)|$.  Above $\sqrt2$, in modified Pr\"ufer variables
with $\rho^2=V$ the Sturm amplitude and $A:=\rho D^{1/4}$ the WKB invariant,
one has the \emph{exact} identity $A'/A=-\frac{D'}{4D}\cos2\theta$.  The
right-hand side is sign-indefinite and vanishes on average; what converts that
average into a bound is the hypothesis, in a form worth stating on its own:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:noturning}
  k(x)^2=\frac{c^2x^2-\chi}{x^2-1}=c^2+\frac{c^2-\chi}{x^2-1}\;>\;c^2 ,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ \emph{$\chi<c^2$ says exactly that there is no turning point outside the
band, and the phase advances at rate at least $c$ everywhere}.  One integration
by parts against $\theta'\ge c-\sqrt2$ then gives
$|\int_{\sqrt2}^X\frac{D'}{4D}\cos2\theta|\le E(c)$ for every $X$.  Feeding in
$A(\sqrt2)\le2^{1/4}c^{1/2}|\Phi(1)|$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude} and
$D(X)^{1/4}>c^{1/2}(X^2-1)^{1/2}$ finishes it.  In the published regime
$\chi>c^2$ there \emph{is} a turning point, at $x=\sqrt\chi/c>1$; that is the
structural reason the neighbouring literature cannot be borrowed, seen from the
other side.

\subsection{Q2: the sum over dilates, and the quantisation point}
\label{sec:q2}

Passing from one prolate function to the summation map means passing from
$\Phi$ to $G(t):=\sum_{m\ge1}\Phi(mt)$, and the triangle inequality is
log-divergent: $\sum_m|\Phi(1)|/(mt)$ diverges harmonically.

\begin{theorem}[\ours; machine-checked given Prop.~4.1 of
\file{dilate-sum.md}]\label{thm:q2}
Let $c>\sqrt2$, let $n$ be even and $0\le\chi_n<c^2$.  Then for every $t>1$ the
series $G(t)=\sum_{m\ge1}\Phi_n(mt)$ converges, and
\[
  \sup_{t>1}\;t\,|G(t)|\;\le\;K_P(c)\,|\Phi_n(1)|,\qquad K_P(c)=O(\log c),
\]
explicitly $K_P=\frac{\pi}{c|\mu_\Phi|}+B_1\big(3+\log\frac{B_2}{B_1}\big)$ with
$B_1=K_1+\frac{2}{c|\mu_\Phi|}$, $B_2=K_1(6c+2^{-1/2})$ and $K_1=K(c)$ the
constant of Theorem~\ref{thm:q1}.  For a combination
$\phi=\sum_mb_m\Phi_{n_m}$ with every $n_m$ even and $\chi_{n_m}<c^2$ the same
holds with $|\Phi_n(1)|$ replaced by $\sum_m|b_m||\Phi_{n_m}(1)|$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent This is the hypothesis \textbf{(P)} that
Proposition~\ref{prop:zetaside}'s decay input (D) needs, and with it (D) holds
with $A^2\ll\lambda K_P^2\Phi(1)^2$.  Numerically $K_P=26.5$ at $c=4\pi$, with a
minimum $17.9$ near $c\approx80$ and never above $27$ in this project's range;
measured, $\sup_t t|G|/|\Phi(1)|=0.94$--$1.05$, attained as $t\to1^+$, so the
proved constant is loose by a factor $17$--$28$ and the truth is $1$.

\paragraph{Theorem~\ref{thm:q1} is not a stepping stone to this; it is the
hypothesis in disguise.}  In the Liouville form $u=\sqrt{x^2-1}\,\Phi$, which
satisfies $u''+(c^2+\epsilon)u=0$ with
$\epsilon=\frac{c^2-\chi}{x^2-1}+\frac{1}{(x^2-1)^2}$ integrable at infinity
(the potential printed here was previously wrong; item~10 of
\S\ref{sec:corrections}), Q1 says exactly that
\emph{$u$ is bounded} --- and bounded $u$ against an integrable perturbation is
what makes the Lagrange coefficients converge and gives the off-band remainder
its second power of $x$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude} alone would not do: it gives
$|u|\le\sqrt{x^2-1}|\Phi(1)|$, which grows.  What (P) needed was \emph{one more
power of $x$}, not one less power of $e^{c}$; the earlier reading of the
obstacle --- that Osipov--Rokhlin's remainder is ``of the same exponential size
as the term being bounded'' --- is true and is not an obstruction, because
$|\Phi(1)|$ sits outside the sum and its size is irrelevant to a harmonic
divergence.

\paragraph{And here is the conceptually interesting half.}  The theorem turns on
the leading off-band term being a \emph{pure sine}:
$\Phi(x)=\frac{a_1\sin(cx)}{x}+O(x^{-2})$ with \textbf{no cosine},
$\beta_\infty=0$, which is the cancellation
$\sum_mm^{-1}\sin(cmt)=\frac{\pi-\gamma}{2}$ for
$\gamma:=ct\bmod2\pi\in(0,2\pi)$, and $0$ at $\gamma=0$: of modulus
$\le\pi/2$ either way, where the triangle inequality gave $+\infty$.

\begin{corollary}[\ours]\label{cor:quantisation}
For a solution of the prolate equation analytic at $x=1$ with
$\beta_\infty\ne0$, $\sup_{t>1}t|G(t)|=+\infty$: the sum diverges
logarithmically as $ct$ approaches $2\pi\Z$, since
$\sum_mm^{-1}\cos(cmt)=-\log|2\sin\tfrac{ct}{2}|$ is unbounded.
\end{corollary}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{So (P) is false for a general solution of the prolate differential
equation and true for the eigenfunctions.}  $\beta_\infty=0$ is a
\emph{quantisation} statement, proved from the finite-Fourier eigenrelation and
\emph{not} from the ODE.  Theorem~\ref{thm:q2} is therefore not analysis that
happens to apply to prolate functions; it is true \emph{because} they are
eigenfunctions, and no argument using the differential equation alone can prove
it.  Any argument that appears to is wrong.
\end{quote}

That is stated at this length deliberately: a statement of Q2 that omits it
invites exactly the ODE-only proof attempt that cannot work.

\paragraph{This subsection is machine-checked from the off-band splitting up, and
that is further than the Pr\"ufer wall would suggest.}  Formalised in Lean~4
(\S\ref{sec:standards}): both Fourier identities --- $\sum_mm^{-1}\sin m\gamma
=\frac{\pi-\gamma}{2}$ and $\sum_mm^{-1}\cos m\gamma=-\log(2\sin\frac\gamma2)$
--- the bound $\pi/2$ on the first including at the resonance $\gamma=0$,
Corollary~\ref{cor:quantisation}'s divergence of the second as $\gamma\to0^+$,
the reduction $\gamma=ct\bmod2\pi$, absolute convergence of the remainder series,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:q2} itself with its explicit constant.  \emph{The two
remainder bounds are no longer hypotheses}: \file{lean/Riemann/Remainder.lean}
proves both, together with the Liouville form and the Lagrange system beneath
them, leaving Theorem~\ref{thm:q1} and the $\beta_\infty=0$ of
Corollary~\ref{cor:quantisation} as the only inputs.  \emph{Formalising that
layer refuted the Liouville potential this paper and the underlying note both
printed} --- item~10 of \S\ref{sec:corrections}; the corrected potential changes
no constant anywhere, and that too is machine-checked.  \emph{The two identities turn out to be
the imaginary and real parts of one complex logarithm}, $-\log(1-e^{i\gamma})$,
which is why they are a dichotomy rather than two facts.

\emph{Why this was reachable when \S\ref{sec:q1} is not} is worth a sentence,
because it is not what one would guess.  The Pr\"ufer angle blocks
\S\ref{sec:q1} and nothing else on this chain: the Lagrange system that
\file{dilate-sum.md} \S3 runs on --- $u=\alpha\sin cx+\beta\cos cx$,
$u'=c(\alpha\cos cx-\beta\sin cx)$ --- determines $\alpha$ and $\beta$ as
\emph{explicit single-valued algebraic functions of $(u,u')$}, with no continuous
branch to construct anywhere.  It is the \emph{polar} form of that substitution,
not the substitution, that mathlib cannot supply.  Reading \S3 as ``the Pr\"ufer
system again'' would have classified Q2 as blocked, and it is not.
\file{lean/README.md} carries the reachability table target by target, including
the two rows (Q3, \eqref{H0}) that are blocked by something else and much larger.

\subsection{Q3: the truncation, and the premise that was false}
\label{sec:q3}

\begin{theorem}[\ours]\label{thm:q3}
With (P) --- Theorems \ref{thm:q1} and \ref{thm:q2} --- and the classical inputs
of \S\ref{sec:zetaside},
\[
  \sum_{\frac12+is\in Z}\big|\F_\mu r(s)\big|^2\;\le\;\Xi(\mu)\,(1-\chi_2(\lambda)),
  \qquad
  \Xi(\mu)=\begin{cases}
    O\big(\mu^{9/2}\log^3\!\mu\big) & \text{\observed}\\[2pt]
    O\big(\mu^{6}\log^3\!\mu\big) & \text{\proved}
  \end{cases}
\]
--- polynomial either way, hence subexponential, which is all
Corollary~\ref{cor:upper} needs.
\end{theorem}

The two values of $\Xi$ differ by exactly one thing and it is worth naming: the
conversion $\Phi(1)^2\mapsto(1-\Lambda_4)$.  The endpoint identity
$\Phi_n(1)^2=c(1-\Lambda_n)(1-\frac{2n+1}{4c}+O(c^{-2}))$ is \observed{} only
(\textbf{Gap G12}); the \proved{} substitute is one-sided in each direction ---
$\Phi_n(1)^2\ge(1-\Lambda_n)/(2K_1^2\Lambda_n)$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:q1} and
\eqref{eq:outofband}, and $\Phi_n(1)^2=O(c^{5/2}(1-\Lambda_n))$ from the
$x^{-2}$ remainder bound --- and costs $\mu^{3/2}$.  \emph{Anyone printing
``unconditional'' is quoting the proved column.}

\paragraph{The mechanism is a truncation of the zero sum, and no fractional
Sobolev theory appears anywhere.}  Split the sum at $|\gamma_\rho|=T_*$ with
$\log T_*=4\pi\mu+O(\log\mu)$.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{Above $T_*$}: use $\F_\mu r=-\F_\mu g$ on $Z$ ---
Proposition~\ref{prop:identity}'s own identity, used again --- and the bounded
variation of $g$ in the logarithmic variable, giving
$\sum_{|\gamma_\rho|>T_*}|F(s_\rho)|^2\ll\lambda V_*^2\log T_*/T_*$.  This is
below the target because $\Phi(1)^2\ge(1-\Lambda_4)/(2K_1^2C_*^2)$ is bounded
below, which is where Theorem~\ref{thm:q1} enters a second time.
\item \emph{Below $T_*$}: apply Proposition~\ref{prop:zetaside} with the weight
$\log^3(3+|t|)$ \emph{frozen} at $\log^3(4+T_*)=O(\mu^3)$ before integrating.
With no $t$-dependent factor left inside, Plancherel on horizontal lines applies
as it stands and nothing about the smoothness of $R$ is ever needed.  The same
truncation caps the $\sigma\to-\tfrac12$ edge at $\eta_*\asymp c_0/\mu$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The whole cost is that $\mu^3$, and it is forced by the size of the
target rather than by any analysis.

\paragraph{An error in our own working notes, recorded because the first draft
of this paper carried it too.}  The route above replaced one that does not
exist, and the reason it does not is worth stating.  The earlier plan was to
absorb the $\log^3$ weight into a fractional Sobolev norm of $R$, on the premise
that ``$R$ is bounded with a single jump at $v=\log\lambda$ and is smooth to the
right of it'' --- which is the same claim the first draft of this paper made
when it described $r$ as ``smooth on $(0,\lambda^{-1})$ but with a jump at the
endpoint''.  \textbf{That premise is false.}  $R$ jumps at $u=\lambda/N$ for
\emph{every} integer $N>\mu$, of size exactly $|\Phi(1)|N^{-1/2}$, because
$\E\phi(u)=u^{1/2}\sum_{n\le\lambda/u}\phi(nu)$ is a finite sum whose terms
switch on one at a time and $\phi(\lambda)\ne0$; measured against one-sided
limits at $24$ cells, ratio $1.0$ to every printed digit.  Those jumps are the
\emph{same} sawtooth that makes Theorem~\ref{thm:q2} work --- so the two
sections of that note contradicted each other, and the section that used the
sawtooth was the right one.  The input the Sobolev route asks for does not
exist: $R'$ is not a function, and between jumps $|R'|\asymp\mu t|R|$, growing
where (D) decays.  Separately, the edge is \emph{one}-sided
($\sigma\to-\tfrac12$ only) and does not converge in the order originally
proposed, since (ZFR) puts the edge at height $e^{c_0/\eta}$ where the $\log^3$
weight is already $(c_0/\eta)^3$, making ``a further logarithm''
$\int d\eta/\eta$ really $\int d\eta/\eta^4$.  \emph{The two flagged steps were
not independent and could not be costed separately}, which is how they had been
costed.  \proved{} \ours

\subsection{H0: the prolate vector does not degenerate, and there is no
\texorpdfstring{$\zeta$}{zeta} in it}
\label{sec:h0}

\begin{theorem}[\ours, \proved{} modulo \cite{Dunster2017} eq.\ (124)+(107)]
\label{thm:h0}
$\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\|g(\lambda)\|^2
=\|\E\varphi_\infty\|^2=0.219247199549\ldots>0$, with
$\varphi_\infty=\sqrt{\tfrac8{11}}\big(h_4-\sqrt{\tfrac38}\,h_0\big)$.  In
particular \eqref{H0} holds.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The first draft named the wrong object, and this is the correction.}
It glossed \eqref{H0} as a mean value of $|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^2$ against an
explicit weight.  There \emph{is} such an identity ---
$\|\E\phi\|^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int|\Gamma_\R(\tfrac12-is)\zeta(\tfrac12-is)P_\phi(s)|^2ds$
--- and it is correct, but it is one \emph{route} to $\|g\|^2$ and it is the
harder one, because it computes the whole norm when only a lower bound is
wanted.  \textbf{There is no $\zeta$ in \eqref{H0}.}  With $u=\lambda/t$ and
$S(t)=\sum_{n\le t}\Phi(n/t)$,
\[
  \|g\|^2=\int_1^{\mu}\frac{S(t)^2}{t^2}\,dt,
  \qquad
  \|r\|^2=\int_\mu^\infty\frac{S(t)^2}{t^2}\,dt ,
\]
exactly, with every argument $n/t$ \emph{on band}; the subtraction
$\|g\|^2=\|\E\phi\|^2-\|r\|^2$, which is what makes \eqref{H0} look like a
statement about the whole critical line, is never needed.  And
$\|g\|^2\ge\int_A^B|\E\phi|^2d^*u$ for any fixed compact
$[A,B]\subseteq[\lambda^{-1},\lambda]$, where $\E\phi$ is a finite sum of
on-band values of one prolate function; $\int_{1/2}^2$ already recovers
$99.9999\%$ of the limit.

\paragraph{What \eqref{H0} actually requires is that $\phi_\lambda$ does not
degenerate}, i.e.\ that the rescaled prolate vector converges in $L^2(\R)$, with
no mass escaping, to a fixed non-zero function.  Seen from the mean-value side
this is the same thing: the weight $|P_{\phi_\lambda}|^2$ moves with $\lambda$
and $\zeta$ does not, so it is the weight's convergence that decides the
question.  The first draft's own sentence --- ``$P_\phi$ depends on $\lambda$ and
the bound must be uniform'' --- named the difficulty correctly and then attached
it to the wrong object.

\paragraph{Proof, in outline, and where the import enters.}  (i)
$\phi_\lambda\to\varphi_\infty$ in $L^2(\R)$: by Dunster's (124)+(107) the
rescaled $\lambda^{-1/2}\Phi_n(X/\lambda)$ is $A_\lambda[h_n(X)+O(c^{-1}\log c)]$
uniformly on compacta, and the tightness bound $c^2\int y^2\Phi^2\le\chi$ ---
straight from the prolate equation --- stops mass escaping to infinity, forcing
$A_\lambda\to1$.  (ii) The window functional
$f\mapsto(\int_A^B|\E f|^2d^*u)^{1/2}$ is continuous against
$\|f\|_2^{1/2}$ on a fixed window, so it converges.  (iii) Hence
$\liminf\|g\|^2\ge\int_{1/2}^2|\E\varphi_\infty|^2d^*u=0.219246959898\ldots>0$,
\emph{and that alone is what the $-4\pi$ bound needs}.  (iv) The matching upper
bound, giving the limit itself, needs both tails controlled uniformly in
$\lambda$; the small-$u$ tail uses $\phi(0)=\widehat\phi(0)=0$ and
Theorem~\ref{thm:q1} again.  The limit is measured at $\mu=3,5,8,12,20$ and
computed two independent ways agreeing to $22$ digits.  What the theorem does
not give is an explicit $\lambda_0$ beyond which the bound holds: it is a limit
statement, and the finite-$\mu$ values are measured rather than proved
(\textbf{Gap G14}).  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a $\limsup$ and does not need
one.

\paragraph{The import, said once more where it is used.}  Only step (i)'s
Hermite limit is imported: \cite{Dunster2017} eq.\ (124) with (107).  Via DLMF
12.7.2, $U(-n-\tfrac12,z)=e^{-z^2/4}\mathit{He}_n(z)$, his (124) at
$x=X/\lambda$ is the Hermite limit.  His statement and hypotheses were read in
the source; the coalescing-turning-point theory his error bounds rest on was
not opened, and neither was Arscott~1964 (his~[1], p.~186), which is what
(107)'s $O(\gamma^{-1})$ rests on through his eq.~(27) --- so the external
chain here is \textbf{two citations deep, not one}.  \textbf{Gap G13.}
Everything else in the proof --- the window identity, the
localisation, the continuity of $\E$ on a compact window, the tightness bound,
and the non-vanishing of $\E\varphi_\infty$ --- is proved here.

\paragraph{His hypothesis, in his strength, and the region it covers.}  It is
\emph{not} $\lambda_{\mathrm D}=\chi-c^2<0$, which an earlier draft of this
paper printed as ``exactly $\chi_n<c^2$''.  It is eq.~(29),
$0\le\sigma=\sqrt{\chi_n}/c\le\sigma_0<1$ with $\sigma_0$ fixed
(\S\ref{sec:whose} tabulates $\sigma$).  It holds at indices $0,2,4,6$
throughout our range and at index~$8$ only above $\mu^*_8=2.1168720\ldots$,
where it fails outright: $\sigma=1.01988$ at $\mu=2$, and $\sigma=0.883$
already at $\mu=3$.  \emph{Step (i) is untouched by this}, and the reason is
structural rather than lucky: it is a $\lambda\to\infty$ limit at \emph{fixed}
$n$, and $\sigma_n(c)\to0$ for fixed $n$, so (29) holds for every $c>c^*_n$ and
the limit is taken well inside it.  No step of Theorem~\ref{thm:h0} evaluates
(124) at $(\mu,n)=(2,8)$.  The finite-$\mu$ values of \textbf{G14} are
measurements and not applications of (124), which is why the failure of (29)
does not reach them either.  \emph{The one cell where (29) fails is the one
cell where Theorem~\ref{thm:q1}'s hypothesis fails, for the reason given at
\S\ref{sec:q1}: it is the same inequality at its boundary.}

\paragraph{The import is tested, and it is not broken.}  Both equations have
been evaluated in our regime against prolate eigenfunctions built by an
eigensolver that has never seen Dunster, at $40$ working digits, over
$c=4\pi,\dots,24\pi$ and $n=0,2,4,6,8$.  \emph{Both hold.}  (124): the quantity
it bounds --- the discrepancy measured against the envelope of
$U(-\tfrac12a,\hat\rho\sqrt{2c})$, which is the form in which the error is

(107): $a=\chi_n/c=2n+1+O(\gamma^{-1})$ holds at every cell, and the
$O(\gamma^{-1})$ is \emph{pinned},
$\chi_n=(2n+1)c-\frac{(2n+1)^2+5}{8}+O(c^{-1})$, one formula with no free
parameter fitting five indices at once with its own residual converging ---
which puts the $\chi_n=(2n+1)c+O(1)$ that step (i) consumes beyond plausible.

been read as such.  The endpoint conclusion is unchanged; what it is evidence

\emph{What the test does and does not change.}  \textbf{A numerical check is not
a proof.}  G13 remains an external dependency, imported and not re-derived, and

\subsection{The house rule, applied to the chain}
\label{sec:chainsignblind}

Every statement in \S\S\ref{sec:notH1}--\ref{sec:h0} is \textbf{sign-blind}, and
that is the correct answer rather than a defect.  \eqref{H1} and its replacement
are bounds on $\sum_\rho|\F_\mu r|^2$, a sum of squared moduli, which cannot
distinguish $\QW_\lambda$ from $-\QW_\lambda$ and was never going to.
Theorems \ref{thm:q1}, \ref{thm:q2} and Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude} contain no
zeta function at all.  Theorem~\ref{thm:h0} is a statement about the
non-degeneracy of a prolate vector.  Theorem~\ref{thm:main} bounds $|s(\mu)|$
from above; the left half of $0<s\le\cdots$ is untouched by any of it, and is
Theorem~\ref{thm:boundary}.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{So the chain closing leaves the thesis exactly where it was, and makes
stating it matter more.}  What was conditional is now proved, and what was
proved is that no part of this machinery can produce a direction.  The
improvement is real, and it is entirely within the magnitude half --- the half
\S\ref{sec:houserule} classifies as sign-blind on every row.  An unconditional
theorem is precisely the point at which a reader stops after the first sentence,
which is why the label is attached here and again in \S\ref{sec:boundary}
rather than left to the summary.
\end{quote}

\section{The boundary: a lower bound of any shape implies RH}
\label{sec:boundary}
% ==========================================================================

A rate is two-sided.  \S\ref{sec:unconditional} proved one half of it
unconditionally.  The remaining half is that $s(\mu)$ does not decay
\emph{faster} than $e^{-4\pi\mu}$, i.e.\ a lower bound $s(\mu)\ge\kappa(\mu)>0$
for all $\mu$ --- and \emph{that} half is not open in the sense the other one
was.  The asymmetry is the point of this section, and it is worth naming
before the argument: what closed did so because it is a bound on a magnitude,
and what remains carries the arithmetic precisely because it is not.

Connes states the equivalence himself (\file{rhready.tex:1145}): ``By the result
of Andr\'e Weil discussed in \S\texttt{sectweilpos}, the positivity of
$\QW_\lambda$ for all $\lambda>1$ is equivalent to RH''; Connes--Consani state
the same at \file{Spectraltriples.tex:169}. \proved{} Therefore:

\begin{theorem}[\ours{} as an observation, from Weil and \cite{Connes2026}]
\label{thm:boundary}
Any theorem of the form $\min(s^+(\mu),s^-(\mu))\ge\kappa(\mu)>0$ for all $\mu$
implies RH.  A theorem that additionally pins the rate is strictly stronger,
since RH gives positivity with no rate at all.
\end{theorem}

Two places one might hope to slip through, and neither works.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Restricting to large $\mu$ does not help.}  The supports are
nested, so $\QW_\Lambda\ge0$ implies $\QW_\lambda\ge0$ for every
$\lambda<\Lambda$: a lower bound valid for all $\mu\ge\mu_0$ already gives
positivity for \emph{all} $\lambda$.
\item \textbf{Dropping the sign does help --- and that is the tell.}  The
statement $|s(\mu)|=e^{-4\pi\mu+o(\mu)}$, with no claim that $s>0$, does not
formally imply RH.  But it is invariant under $\QW_\lambda\mapsto-\QW_\lambda$,
and is therefore compatible with the form being negative on the near-radical at
every $\mu$.  \emph{The only version of ``the rate is $4\pi$'' that says anything
about the sign of the Weil form is the version that contains RH.}
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:permanent}
The \emph{two-sided} $4\pi$ rate cannot be made an unconditional theorem --- not
for want of technique; the statement contains RH.  And no computation can change
that: a wider grid or a converged truncation sharpens the fitted constant, but it
cannot supply a lower bound at a $\mu$ it has not computed, and the statement
quantifies over all $\mu$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Read Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:permanent} together,
because separately each of them misleads.}  The upper half of the rate is now a
theorem with no hypotheses; the lower half is RH.  \emph{Those two facts have the
same cause.}  The upper bound was reachable because it bounds
$\sum_\rho|\F_\mu r|^2$, a sum of squared moduli, by a concentration defect ---
every step a statement about magnitude.  The lower bound is unreachable for
exactly the reason the upper one was reachable: there is nothing in the prolate
mechanism that knows which way the inequality goes.  So the closure of
\S\ref{sec:unconditional} is not progress toward the lower bound and does not
narrow the distance to it by any amount; it is the completion of the half that
was never the difficulty.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The house rule predicted this.}  The mechanism of \S\ref{sec:objects}
--- the radical contains $\operatorname{ran}\E$, the near-radical is the failure
of a double support condition, the size is a concentration defect --- is entirely
invariant under $\QW_\lambda\mapsto-\QW_\lambda$.  A kernel is a kernel either
way; a concentration defect is a magnitude.  So the prolate story explains why
$|s|$ is minuscule and says nothing whatever about why $s>0$.  It is exactly that
sign-blindness which forces the lower bound to carry the whole arithmetic
content, and RH is the arithmetic content.  \emph{The standing test located the
obstruction before the analysis did.}
\end{quote}

For the record, the same two-sidedness has a numerical shadow that is worth
naming, because it is a check anyone can run: the three-parameter fit
$\log_{10}s(\mu)=-A\mu+B\log_{10}\mu+D$ over $\mu=5,\dots,12$ gives
$A=5.4635\pm0.052$, $B=5.322\pm0.96$, $D=6.589\pm0.44$, against the index-4
predictions $4\pi/\log10=5.45751$ ($0.12\sigma$), $9/2$ ($0.86\sigma$) and
$\log_{10}(2^{14}\sqrt2\pi^5/3)=6.373563$ ($0.49\sigma$) --- three parameters,
none free.  The index-0 reading predicts $B=\tfrac12$, which is $5.0\sigma$ away.
That is evidence for the \emph{identification}; by Corollary~\ref{cor:permanent}
it is not, and can never be, evidence for the theorem.

\section{The obstruction, sharpened}
\label{sec:sharpening}
% ==========================================================================

This is the deliverable.  Combining \S\ref{sec:deficit} and \S\ref{sec:scale}: a
proof of $\QW_\lambda\ge0$ must establish that, on the near-radical direction,
\[
  \sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}(v_\mu)\;=\;\Big(\sum_pW_p\Big)(v_\mu)\;+\;s(\mu),
  \qquad
  \sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}(v_\mu)\approx0.025\log\mu,
  \qquad
  s(\mu)\approx\kappa\,(1-\chi_2(\lambda)),
\]
with $s>0$.  In words: \emph{two computable quantities of size
$\approx0.025\log\mu$, one archimedean and one a finite sum over $p^m<\mu$,
agree to about $5.2$ decimal digits per unit of $\mu$ over the computed range ---
at a rate consistent with $4\pi/\log10=5.4575$ --- and the sign of the
discrepancy is always the same one.}

Three consequences.  Each is a constraint on \emph{method}, not a restatement of
difficulty.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep4pt
\item \textbf{No fixed-relative-error argument can work.}  Any bound on either
side accurate to a fixed relative error $\varepsilon$, or to a fixed power
$\mu^{-k}$, loses at
$\mu\gtrsim(k\log\mu+\log(1/\varepsilon))/(4\pi/\log10)$.  The required relative
accuracy is $e^{-4\pi\mu}/(0.025\log\mu)$: not a constant, and not polynomial.

\item \textbf{No prime can be dropped and no tail estimated.}  Each $p^m<\mu$
individually carries $0.10$--$0.84$, fifty-three orders above the residue
(\S\ref{sec:loadbearing}), and the cost is not ordered by the size of the prime.
The sum is not dominated by its head, and the standard move of controlling
$\sum_{p>P}$ crudely is unavailable at every $P<\mu$.

\item \textbf{The difficulty is not magnitude but cancellation, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:deficit} is what makes that precise.}  The deficit is bounded by
$5.3721834\ldots$ for all $\mu$ --- a fixed, small, explicit constant.  Nothing
on the archimedean side diverges.  What has to be proved is an
exact-to-$e^{-4\pi\mu}$ agreement between two bounded quantities, which is a
statement about the arithmetic of the primes and not about the size of anything.
\end{enumerate}

Read the other way, this is also an explanation of \emph{why} Connes--Consani's
near-radical construction is the right conceptual object: the near-radical is
exactly the subspace on which the two sides are forced into this agreement, and
its dimension $1+\nu(\mu)\sim2\mu$ is the number of independent such agreements
that $\mu$ demands at once.

And it is why the sharpening stops here rather than continuing.  A fourth
constraint would have to say something about the \emph{direction} of the
discrepancy; by \S\ref{sec:boundary} any such statement, of any shape, is RH.

\paragraph{What \S\ref{sec:unconditional} adds to the three constraints, and
what it does not.}  It makes the ``$\approx5.2$ decimal digits per unit of
$\mu$'' half of the sentence above a theorem rather than a measurement: the
upper bound $s(\mu)=O(\mu^{21/2}\log^3\!\mu\,e^{-4\pi\mu})$ says that the two
sides \emph{do} agree at least that well, for every $\mu$, with no hypothesis.
Constraint~1 therefore no longer rests on a fit.  It changes constraints 2 and 3
not at all --- those are about the primes and about
Theorem~\ref{thm:deficit} --- and it changes nothing about the direction of the
discrepancy, which is the whole content and is RH.

Read the table.  \emph{Every sign-blind row that says anything about the form is
part of the prolate/magnitude machinery} --- the objects, the notation, the
index, the indefiniteness of the prime terms, the commuting pair at a finite
place, the identity, the rate, the phase-space picture, and the whole of
\S\ref{sec:unconditional}.  (The last row says nothing about the form: it
records that two computations agree and that two printed expressions do not,
which no involution of $\QW_\lambda$ can touch.)

\emph{Seven of the rows above are new since the first draft --- five with the
chain of \S\ref{sec:unconditional}, two with the audit and the formalisation of
this revision --- and every one of them is sign-blind.}  That is the strongest
form of the thesis available: an entire
chain of theorems was added, it turned a conditional result into an
unconditional one, and it moved not a single row into the ``false for $-W$''
column.  Then the one external input was tested and the elementary layer was
machine-checked, and \emph{that} moved no row either, which is the expected
answer twice over: a test of an asymptotic and a compiled proof are both
confidence in a magnitude.  The sign-bearing rows are exactly the ones they were.
\emph{Every sign-bearing row is an inequality about $\lambda_{\min}$ of the
actual form}, or the one proved square at the archimedean place.  There is no row
in which the prolate mechanism produces a direction, and this is not for want of
looking: the classification was run on the claims as they stood.

The same test applied to the underlying note on the rate gave three sign-blind
claims out of six --- the index, the identity, and the rate itself --- with the
three sign-bearing ones each an inequality about $\lambda_{\min}$.  That is the
honest result and it is \emph{the argument}, not an embarrassment: it is what
turns ``we did not manage to prove positivity'' into ``this mechanism cannot
prove positivity, and here is the test that shows it''.

\paragraph{Two warnings to whoever quotes this paper.}
``$s(\mu)$ is governed by $1-\chi_2$'' is by itself invariant under
$W\mapsto-W$; it is sign-bearing only as ``$0<s(\mu)\le\cdots$'', and the left
half of that is RH.  Likewise ``the two halves agree to $5.46\mu$ digits'' is
sign-blind on its own, and is admissible only as part of ``\ldots and the
archimedean half is the positive one''.

\section{Gaps, and open problems}
\label{sec:gaps}

Recorded rather than filled; several were discovered while assembling this paper
and are not closed by it.  The list is in three blocks: what is open, what was
open in the first draft and is now closed (kept, with what was actually proved
spelled out, so that neither the closure nor its caveats can be lost), and the
one row that is not open because it is RH.  Numbering is stable across the two
drafts: G5 and G6 kept their numbers when they closed, and the items the closure

\paragraph{Open.}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{0.72\textwidth}@{}}

\textbf{G1} & Turn Prop.~\ref{prop:witness} into a theorem: an explicit family of

running the same code again is not a check of it.  Five of those numbers, the
load-bearing ones, have since been recomputed from the definitions in
\cite{CC2023} by a second implementation of ours,
\file{verify_independent_recheck.py}, written without opening either of the first
two: they were read only afterwards, once every number had been computed and
written down, which is the order the exercise depends on --- a reimplementation
written after reading the original inherits its slips silently and then agrees
with it.  \file{independent-recheck.md} reports the whole of it.  This subsection
gives the outcome, then what is independent in it, then what is not, the last of
these at the same length as the first because it is the part a reader cannot
reconstruct from the agreement.  Nothing here was re-run on this editing pass.

\paragraph{Outcome.}  All five load-bearing numbers reproduce, to every digit
printed here: the bound $|2\vartheta'(0)|=5.3721834\ldots$ by two routes and all
ten rows of the saturation table (\S\ref{sec:deficit}); all nine rows of $D$,
$R$, $R-D$, $R/D$, including the shape of the oscillation; all eight rows of the
reversed-sign split, signs and magnitudes (\S\ref{sec:thirddirection}); the
prime-omission table (\S\ref{sec:loadbearing}); the three fitted parameters,
their standard errors and all four $\sigma$ comparisons (\S\ref{sec:boundary});
and the prolate defects with both ratio columns (\S\ref{sec:confront}), giving
$s/(1-\chi_2)\in[7.6074,12.9609]$ and growth by a factor $56.16$ against
$1-\Lambda_0$, at $N=100$.  The $N$-convergence of \S\ref{sec:theirnumber}
reproduces to seven digits at all four truncations.  Two external anchors hold:
$\lambda_{\min}(\sigma^{\mathrm{arch}})=0.0013310$ at $\mu=2$ against
Connes--Consani's $\sim0.00133$ (\file{:551}), and Slepian's $\Lambda_0(1)$.

\paragraph{What is independent, stated precisely.}  The precise version is
checkable and ``independently verified'' is not.  The second implementation
shares with the first
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{no arithmetic library} --- Python's \texttt{decimal} (libmpdec,
decimal radix) against mpmath (binary radix over Python ints), so a rounding or
radix bug in one is not a bug in the other;
\item \textbf{no transcendental function} --- $\pi$, $\log$, $\exp$,
$\sin/\cos$, $\gamma$, $\psi$ and $\Re\psi$ of a complex argument are
implemented there from their series, where the original calls mpmath for every
one of them;
\item \textbf{no eigensolver} --- Householder tridiagonalisation and
Sturm-sequence bisection, which returns a \emph{bracket} whose endpoints are
separately checkable by an inertia count, against the original's iteration;
\item and, for the archimedean boundary term, \textbf{no method} --- it is
derived there in closed form as the rank-one matrix $W_{0,2}(n,m)=2v_nv_m$ with
$v_n=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\xi_n(x)e^{x/2}dx$, where the original computes that block
by quadrature.  That closed form is also what makes step (ii) of the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:deficit} --- the boundary term is a square --- visible in the
basis rather than asserted.
\end{itemize}
Two checks there have no counterpart in the original: the prolate normalisation
against the trace sum rule $\sum_n\Lambda_n(c)=2c/\pi$, to $58$ digits at $c=5$,
which pins the one factor a derivation of $\Lambda_n$ from the finite Fourier
transform could get wrong; and the convolution table at \file{:444} re-derived
twice, once symbolically and once by brute-force numerical convolution of the
basis functions themselves.

\paragraph{What is \emph{not} independent, and is therefore corroborated by none
of the above.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{The definitions.}  Both implementations read the same paper.
\emph{A shared misreading of \cite{CC2023} is invisible to this exercise}, and
under one every agreement above is worthless.  That is irreducible rather than a
defect of effort, and it is why the objects are tabulated by source line, here
and in the note, so that the reading itself can be disagreed with.  The only fix
is a third party who reads \cite{CC2023} without reading us; this is not a
substitute for one.
\item \textbf{Proposition \texttt{Hilbert} (\file{:289}) and eq.\
(\texttt{thetaprime}) (\file{:261}).}  Used as theorems by both implementations
and re-proved by neither.  The \emph{normalisation} of the second is now tested
numerically and holds to $18$ digits (\S\ref{sec:bound}); the proposition and
its proof are still taken from the paper by everyone here.
\item \textbf{The quadrature scheme.}  Composite Gauss--Legendre with
Newton-refined Legendre roots was chosen independently for the recheck and turned
out to be the same scheme the original uses.  That is a real limit on what the
agreement of the two matrices establishes, and \emph{arriving at the same scheme
independently is not the same thing as being independent of the scheme}.  What
does differ is the panel count, the node count and the grouping of the singular
integrand; varying panels and nodes moves $D(8)$ in none of $36$ decimals.
\item \textbf{The route to the prolate eigenvalues.}  The Legendre (Bouwkamp)
expansion and the identity $\Lambda_n=c\,d_0^2/(\pi\psi_n(0)^2)$ are the same in
both --- derived rather than copied, but the natural ones.  This is the weakest
independence claim of the exercise; the sum rule pins the normalisation and does
not pin the expansion.
\item \textbf{Fuchs' asymptotic} is quoted by both and derived by neither, as
\S\ref{sec:standards} already records.
\item \textbf{Not attempted at all}: the odd sector (\textbf{G7}); any re-proof
of Proposition \texttt{Hilbert}; and the mantissa of Connes--Consani's
$2.389\times10^{-48}$, which \S\ref{sec:theirnumber} leaves unverified and which
the recheck leaves unverified in exactly the same way.
\item \textbf{Not covered, of this paper's other numbers}: the geometric
extrapolation in $N$ of \S\ref{sec:truncbias} and the extrapolated ratios
$7.1$--$9.9$ that \S\ref{sec:whose} reports; the Fuchs-against-exact accuracy
ratios of \S\ref{sec:fuchs}; the one-bit discriminator of
\S\ref{sec:convention}; and everything in \S\ref{sec:onesign} and
\S\ref{sec:noplacewise}, which belong to a different note and a different
script.  Those stand where \S\ref{sec:provenance} leaves them --- computed once,
by the note that owns them.
\item \textbf{Not covered, and the whole of it postdates the recheck}: every
number in \S\ref{sec:unconditional}.  The second implementation was written
before that chain existed and does not touch it.  What partially substitutes is
that the chain's numbers are mostly \emph{constants in theorems} rather than
inputs to conclusions --- and one genuine cross-check exists inside it, in the
opposite direction from the usual one: Theorem~\ref{thm:q1}'s measured amplitude
$\sqrt{2/\pi}=0.797885$ is exactly the constant in Osipov--Rokhlin's published
off-band leading term, arrived at here from an unrelated computation, so each
confirms the other.
\end{itemize}

and not about $s$.  It is a reproduction and not a verification --- the status
\S\ref{sec:confront} and Corollary~\ref{cor:upper} already give it, unchanged by
having been reproduced.

\subsection{An erratum in \texorpdfstring{\cite{CC2023}}{CC2023}, and what does
not depend on it}
\label{sec:erratum}

This is here as a courtesy to a reader checking our arithmetic against
Connes--Consani's, and for nothing else.

Their Lemma \texttt{w02} gives closed forms for
$\widehat F(i/2)+\widehat F(-i/2)$ with $F(x)=\theta(\log x)$ and
$\theta=\xi_m\star\xi_n^*$.  The even-sector form as printed
(\file{Spectraltriples.tex:474}, eq.\ \texttt{h02ev}) is
\[
  \frac{8\,e^{-L/2}(e^{L/2}-1)^2L^3}{(L^2+16\pi^2m^2)(L^2+16\pi^2n^2)} .
\]
Carrying out the substitution the surrounding proof describes --- integrating
$(\theta(t)+\theta(-t))(e^{t/2}+e^{-t/2})$ over $[0,L]$ against the convolution
table at \file{:444}, from which the formula is derived four lines later ---
gives the same expression with $16$ in place of $8$.  Evaluated at $L=\log5$ for
$(n,m)=(1,2),(3,3),(4,7)$ the ratio of the two is $2.000000000000000$; the
odd-sector form at \file{:478} agrees with the same table at ratio
$1.000000000000000$.  Three routes give the $16$: term-by-term integration of the
\file{:444} even entry against $2\cosh(y/2)$; the rank-one form
$W_{0,2}(n,m)=2v_nv_m$ of \S\ref{sec:recheck}, which is the boundary term
$2\Re(\widehat f(\tfrac i2)\overline{\widehat f(-\tfrac i2)})$ of
\eqref{eq:hilbert} written in the basis, so that the factor is fixed by
Proposition \texttt{Hilbert} itself; and the $(0,0)$ entry, where
$\int_0^L(L-y)2\cosh(y/2)\,dy=4K$ exactly by parts with
$K=e^{L/2}-2+e^{-L/2}$. \observed{} \ours{} (elementary; the three routes are
written out in \file{independent-recheck.md})

\paragraph{The dependency, said out loud.}  \emph{Nothing in this paper depends
on the erratum being right.}  Neither of our implementations uses
(\texttt{h02ev}): both build $W_{0,2}$ from the table at \file{:444}, one by
quadrature and one from the closed form above, and the two agree.  What this
paper does depend on is our reading of that table --- if the table were being
read wrongly, the block $W_{0,2}$ and with it the whole of
\S\ref{sec:deficit} would move --- and that dependency is the shared-reading
exposure of \S\ref{sec:recheck}, not this one.  A reader who checks our
$\sigma^{\mathrm{arch}}$ against \file{:474} as printed will find it a factor
of~$2$ away, and that is the only reason this subsection exists.

\subsection{Corrections, recorded rather than absorbed}
\label{sec:corrections}

Ten corrections of ours are folded into the text above.  They are listed here
because a reader weighing the tables should know which way the errors ran, and
because the pattern in them is itself a finding.  The last four are new in this
revision and the last three were each found by a machine: \emph{formalisation has
now found one defect per ticket it has run for}, which is the strongest argument
in this paper for continuing to run it.  \emph{Item~10 is the first of them that
is a wrong proof rather than an understated hypothesis}, and the difference is
worth reading: items~8 and~9 were printed statements weaker than the arguments
beneath them, which re-reading cannot see because the prose is true as far as it
goes; item~10 is a displayed formula that is false, inside a proof whose
conclusion is nonetheless correct.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{A stated strategy read as a companion theorem.}  An early note
cited ``the archimedean place and its semilocal extension'' as though both were
proved.  Only the archimedean place is.  This is the origin of the status
vocabulary in \S\ref{sec:standards}.
\item \textbf{A check against one paper, written as a claim about the
literature.}  A row of the citation audit called the identification of the
controlling scale ``corpus-specific'' on the strength of arXiv:2106.01715.  It is
Connes', in a survey that predated the audit by six months and that the audit
itself named as the place to look.  A scoping defect, not a decayed finding.
\item \textbf{The wrong prolate index, from the nearer formula.}  The rate was
first read off the index-0 Slepian asymptotic, because that was the formula in
the note being continued --- while the correct index was already written into two
other notes of ours, two commits earlier.  Proximity beat provenance, and the
provenance was our own.
\item \textbf{A non-discriminating parameter mistaken for a test.}  The
$0.12\sigma$ agreement of the fitted rate with $4\pi$ was treated as evidence for
the index.  It is not: $e^{-2c}$ has no index dependence, so every candidate
passes.  \S\ref{sec:fuchs}.
\item \textbf{A working note's account of its own obstacle, wrong four times
running.}  This is the pattern the chain of \S\ref{sec:unconditional} produced,
and it is recorded as one item because it happened once per step and always the
same way.  The note that reduced \eqref{H1} to a prolate tail bound also
diagnosed what stood in the way at each stage, and each diagnosis was wrong.
\emph{Q1} was called ``a Bessel-type connection problem at the regular singular
point $x=1$''; it is not, and the singular point is never crossed
(\S\ref{sec:lemma51}).  \emph{Q2}'s obstacle was said to be the exponential size
of an off-band remainder; the real one was an exponent in $x$, and the size was
irrelevant (\S\ref{sec:q2}).  \emph{Q3} was called routine on both its steps;
one step's premise was false and contradicted another section of the same note,
and the two steps were not independent (\S\ref{sec:q3}).  \emph{\eqref{H0}} was
called a mean value of $|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^2$; there is no $\zeta$ in it at
all (\S\ref{sec:h0}).  The first three mispriced a difficulty; the fourth named
the wrong object.  \textbf{The methodological conclusion, since four for four is
not a coincidence: a post-mortem written by whoever hit a wall is evidence about
that person's attempt and not about the wall.}  Every one of the four closures
came from refusing the inherited diagnosis rather than from working on it.
\item \textbf{A grid that excluded the point where the supremum lives, twice.}
A measured constant $\sup_{x>1}x|\Phi(x)|/|\Phi(1)|\le0.79$ was reported and
then quoted downstream as though it were Theorem~\ref{thm:q1}'s constant; the
grid started $0.008$ \emph{above} $x=1$ and the supremum over $x\ge1$ is $1$,
attained at $x=1$.  The same exclusion was then committed one note later, on a
different variable, under-reporting by a factor of two.  Both are corrected;
neither changes a conclusion; the pattern is worth more than either incident.
\item \textbf{An imported theorem's hypothesis restated in the strength that
suited us.}  This paper printed Dunster's standing hypothesis as
$\lambda_{\mathrm D}<0$ and drew the conclusion that it is ``exactly our
$\chi_n<c^2$'' --- at \S\ref{sec:whose}, \S\ref{sec:standards},
\S\ref{sec:h0} and \textbf{G13}, i.e.\ everywhere the import is named.  His
hypothesis is eq.~(29), $\sqrt{\chi_n}/c\le\sigma_0<1$ with $\sigma_0$ fixed,
which is strictly stronger and \emph{fails at one cell of the grid this project
computes on}, index~$8$ below $\mu=2.1169$.  \emph{This is the only correction
in this list that was a false statement about someone else's paper rather than
a misdescription of our own work}, which is why it went out on its own.  Three
things about how it survived.  It was not a failure to read the source: the
underlying note \emph{does} quote (29) correctly further down, and it was the
summary line at the top --- the one a reader carries away --- that had the
weaker form.  The numeric check attached to it, ``$\chi_n/c^2\le0.64$, so
$\sigma\le0.8$ with room'', was quoted from a table measured at $n\le4$ and
applied to a chain that uses index~$8$; both its endpoints are identifiable
$n\le4$ cells.  And the failing cell was \emph{already printed in this
repository}, by \file{verify_q1.py}, as a failure of our own
Theorem~\ref{thm:q1}'s hypothesis --- the same cell, the same inequality
(\S\ref{sec:q1}).  Nothing downstream moves: Theorem~\ref{thm:h0} consumes the
import only in a limit at fixed $n$ (\S\ref{sec:h0}).
\item \textbf{One hypothesis doing two jobs, found by a machine.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:amplitude} was stated with ``$\Phi$ analytic at $x=1$'' and
remarked to need only ``boundedness near $x=1$''.  Formalising it showed the
remark names one of the two things analyticity was supplying: the proof needs
$\Phi'$ bounded near $1^+$ \emph{and} $\Phi$ right-continuous at $1$, the
second for the endpoint value itself.  \emph{The proof was not wrong and no
result moves} --- the application is to an entire function --- but the stated
weakening was, and \S\ref{sec:q1}'s hypothesis paragraph had inherited it.  It
is worth its place in this list for how it was found rather than for what it
cost: the first lemma formalised, on the first ticket, and by an artefact that
cannot be persuaded that a hypothesis is obviously enough.
\item \textbf{A corollary missing the hypothesis that carries its own
conclusion, found by the same machine.}  Corollary~\ref{cor:upper} was printed
with two hypotheses --- \eqref{H0}, and the $\Xi(\mu)(1-\chi_2)$ bound --- and
concluded $\limsup\mu^{-1}\log s(\mu)\le-4\pi$.  It does not follow: both stated
hypotheses are subexponential-factor statements, \emph{neither mentions $4\pi$},
and the rate has to arrive with $1-\chi_2$.  The missing hypothesis is now
\textbf{(F)}, $\mu^{-1}\log(1-\chi_2(\lambda))\to-4\pi$, and it is Fuchs'
asymptotic \cite{Fuchs1964} --- a source this paper lists under ``not read''.

\emph{No conclusion moves and ``unconditional'' is unaffected}: (F) holds, it is
classical, \S\ref{sec:fuchs} states it and checks the constant to $100$ digits,
and the corollary was always going to be applied with it in hand.  What moves is
the \textbf{ledger}: Theorem~\ref{thm:main}'s status line named one import and
there are two, and the more interesting half of that is which one supplies the
headline number --- \emph{the $4\pi$ is Fuchs', not ours}, and no theorem of
\S\ref{sec:unconditional} produces it.  This is consistent with, and sharper
than, item~4 above: the rate cannot discriminate the index because the rate was
never ours to discriminate with.

Two things about how it survived.  It is a \emph{structural} omission and not a
transcription slip --- the Lean statement cannot be written down without (F),
which is exactly why formalising found it on the first attempt at the corollary
--- and it survived because the paper supplies (F) two hundred lines earlier, in
a subsection about a different question (\S\ref{sec:fuchs}, on whether the rate
discriminates the index), so every reader who checked the corollary against the
paper found the fact present and the hypothesis list unexamined.
\item \textbf{A false formula inside a correct proof: the Liouville potential.}
\S\ref{sec:q2} and \file{notes/dilate-sum.md} Lemma~3.1 both printed the
Liouville transform of the prolate equation as
\[
  u=\sqrt{x^2-1}\,\Phi,\qquad u''+(c^2+\epsilon)u=0,\qquad
  \epsilon=\frac{c^2-\chi+1}{x^2-1}.
\]
The potential is wrong.  The true one is
$\epsilon=\frac{c^2-\chi}{x^2-1}+\frac{1}{(x^2-1)^2}$; the note's proof divides
$\tfrac12(p^{-1/2}p')'$ by $p^{-1/2}$ where the preceding line licenses $p^{1/2}$,
and with $p=x^2-1$ that turns an $(x^2-1)^{-2}$ into an $(x^2-1)^{-1}$.  A control
needing no machine: at $c=\chi=0$ the equation $((x^2-1)\Phi')'=0$ has the
solution $\Phi\equiv1$, so $u=\sqrt{x^2-1}$ and $u''=-(x^2-1)^{-3/2}$, which the
true potential annihilates and the printed one does not.

\emph{No constant anywhere moves, and this is checked rather than argued.}  The
potential is consumed at exactly one point --- the tail bound $\int_X^\infty
\epsilon$ that makes the Lagrange coefficients converge --- and the true
$\epsilon$ satisfies it with \emph{more} room than the printed one
($(2c^2+\tfrac23)/X$ against $2(c^2+1)/X$, both below the $3c^2/X$ actually
used).  So Theorem~\ref{thm:q2}'s $B_1$, $B_2$ and $K_P(c)$, and every number in
the tables of \S\ref{sec:q2}, stand as printed.

Three things about how it survived.  The two potentials \emph{agree to leading
order as $x\to\infty$}, which is the regime the whole argument lives in, so no
asymptotic sanity check separates them.  The numerical companion
(\file{notes/verify_q2.py}) checks the conclusions of Lemmas 3.2--3.3 and
Proposition~4.1 --- $\alpha,\beta$ extracted algebraically from $(u,u')$, and
the two remainder bounds --- \emph{none of which evaluates $\epsilon$}, so a grid
could not have caught it either.  And the previous formalisation ticket stopped
one lemma short: it took the off-band splitting as a hypothesis, so Lemma~3.1 was
never presented to the compiler.  \emph{The defect was reachable only by
formalising the step nobody had a reason to doubt}, which is the argument for
formalising downward rather than outward.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Three further corrections, all cosmetic and none of them of this kind,
came out of the recheck and are recorded with it in \S\ref{sec:recheck}.

\paragraph{The claim in this paper that would do the most damage if wrong} is the
ratio table of \S\ref{sec:confront}, because \S\ref{sec:index},
\S\ref{sec:identity} and \S\ref{sec:boundary} are all readings of it.  It has two
exposures: the prolate solver (guarded by an independent eigensolver and by
precision-stability checks), and the possibility that the near-flatness of
$s/(1-\chi_2)$ over $\mu=5..12$ is an accident of a short range --- eight integer
points spanning a factor $2.4$ in $\mu$, on a quantity that varies by $37$ orders
of magnitude across them.  The second is not guarded and cannot be at this cost.
It is, however, guarded against implementation error in the way described in
\S\ref{sec:recheck}, and against a shared misreading of \cite{CC2023} not at all.

The next most exposed was Theorem~\ref{thm:deficit}, whose only soft joint is the
normalisation of Connes--Consani's Proposition \texttt{Hilbert} --- a factor of
$2$ there doubles the constant.  \emph{That exposure is now closed}, and it is
recorded rather than deleted because a caveat that was real and was then
discharged is part of the evidence.  It was closed by test and not by argument:
eq.\ (\texttt{thetaprime}) is confirmed to $18$ digits on a test function whose
transform decays like $t^{-5}$, so that no asymptotic tail has to be supplied and
the residual is measurably the cutoff rather than a constant
(\S\ref{sec:bound}).  There is no extra factor of $2$.  What is still taken on
trust, and marked as such, is Proposition \texttt{Hilbert} itself: its
normalisation has been tested, its proof has not been re-proved by anyone here.

\paragraph{And the claim in \S\ref{sec:unconditional} that would do the most
damage if wrong} is not one of its theorems --- those are self-contained, and
between them they contain no zeta function, no numerical input and no sign --- but
the import of \cite{Dunster2017} eq.\ (124)+(107) at
Theorem~\ref{thm:h0}(i), \textbf{G13}.  If that asymptotic or its regime were
misread, \eqref{H0} would revert to a hypothesis and Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
would revert to conditional; nothing else in the paper would move, since no
other section uses it.  \emph{It was misread once, in exactly the way this
paragraph is about}: the hypothesis was converted into our symbols as
$\lambda_{\mathrm D}=\chi-c^2<0\Leftrightarrow\chi_n<c^2$, and that
equivalence is true but is not his hypothesis, which is eq.~(29)
(\S\ref{sec:corrections}, item~7).  Its guards are now four, and they are of
different kinds.  The statement and hypotheses were read in the source, with
the equation located by \LaTeX{} label rather than by printed number.  The
hypothesis is checked \emph{cell by cell} against the grid this project
